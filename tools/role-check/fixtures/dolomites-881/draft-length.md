# Environmental Impact Assessment: Improved Irrigation Technologies in the Indus Basin

## Executive Summary

The project will install Improved Irrigation Technologies in the Indus Basin of Pakistan. It replaces traditional surface irrigation with drip and sprinkler systems in order to raise agricultural productivity and to reduce groundwater depletion across the basin.

The new systems can run on several energy sources: subsidized electricity, solar energy, and diesel engines. The choice of energy source matters for the environmental outcome, because diesel pumping is already the main source of CO2 emissions from irrigation in the region.

The assessment identifies four main environmental pressures at the site. Groundwater is severely depleted by excessive abstraction. CO2 emissions from energy use are high. Water use efficiency is low because traditional methods lose much of the water they draw. Soil and water quality suffer from the overuse of fertilizers and pesticides.

The proposed mitigation measures follow from those pressures. Efficient irrigation cuts water losses, solar power reduces emissions compared with diesel, and monitoring of air, water, soil, and biodiversity against the national standards continues throughout implementation. Local communities, environmental organizations, and regulators are engaged from the planning stage onward.

## Legal and Regulatory Compliance

The project is subject to the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act of 1997. The Act is the main national law that governs the environmental consequences of projects of this kind.

The project is also subject to the National Environmental Quality Standards. The standards govern air and water quality, waste management, and biodiversity conservation, and they set the limits against which the project's monitoring results are compared.

The Punjab Irrigated-Agriculture Productivity Improvement Project provides specific guidelines for the implementation of Improved Irrigation Technologies in the region. Following these guidelines aligns the project with recognized good practice for installing efficient irrigation.

The permits and licenses obtained for the project specify conditions for environmental protection. The project will meet these conditions as part of its commitment to the applicable environmental standards, and the monitoring programme will record compliance with them.

## Baseline Environmental Conditions

The Indus Basin of Pakistan faces severe groundwater depletion. The main cause is excessive abstraction of groundwater for irrigation.

Energy consumption for pumping produces high CO2 emissions in the region. Most of these emissions come from diesel pumping.

Water use efficiency in the basin is low. Traditional irrigation methods lose a significant share of the water that they draw.

Soil quality is affected by the overuse of fertilizers and pesticides. The same overuse also affects water quality in the area.

Air quality standards are met at the project site. Concentrations of PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, and O3 are within acceptable limits.

Meteorological data describe the local wind patterns and temperature variations. These data support the interpretation of the air quality results.

Water quality is tracked through pH, dissolved oxygen, biochemical oxygen demand, chemical oxygen demand, and turbidity. Concentrations of pollutants are taken from monitoring data.

Biodiversity assessments record the diversity of flora and fauna, habitat maps, and species-specific data. Together they describe the ecological interactions that the project must protect.
