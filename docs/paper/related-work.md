# Related work

Prior work supplies three pieces of the problem addressed here: a cognitive account of composing, staged systems for long-form generation, and tools that support human revision. The present work connects those pieces at the level of an executable, inspectable process while keeping product evaluation separate from process analysis.

## Cognitive process theories of writing

Flower and Hayes' Cognitive Process Theory of Writing[^1] replaces a stage account of composing with a model of interacting processes. The theory distinguishes Planning, Translating, and Reviewing under the control of a Monitor, and it places those processes in interaction with the task environment and the writer's long-term memory. Planning includes generating ideas, organizing them, and setting goals; Translating turns represented meanings into written language; and Reviewing evaluates and revises both text and plans. The processes can be embedded and re-entered as needed, so revision is not restricted to an end stage.

The theory explains coherence through a hierarchical network of goals that develops during composing. Writers formulate broad rhetorical aims, derive more operational sub-goals, and return to higher-level goals as local work reveals new constraints. The theory also allows writers to regenerate goals when writing changes what they understand about the task. Our process mapping adopts these commitments as observable design targets, while the experiment treats its trace as an operational record rather than evidence of human-like private cognition.

Bereiter and Scardamalia's knowledge-telling versus knowledge-transforming distinction[^2] adds a developmental account of why goal structure matters. Knowledge-telling retrieves content associated with a topic and converts it into prose with limited transformation of the rhetorical problem, whereas knowledge-transforming coordinates content knowledge with the demands of the rhetorical problem. The plugin treats goal development, goal regeneration, and review-triggered changes as the operational locations where such transformation may become visible. The distinction motivates process measures, but the experiment does not infer a writer's cognitive state from a log.

## Large language model long-form writing systems

STORM[^3] is a strong example of a research-to-draft pipeline for long-form generation. The system discovers perspectives, asks simulated perspective-specific questions, curates information, builds an outline, generates an article, and polishes the result. The surveyed implementation separates these modules, and the evaluation reports FreshWiki results and feedback from experienced Wikipedia editors. STORM therefore demonstrates the value of explicit planning and source organization, but its principal organization remains a staged pipeline rather than a Monitor that can re-enter or interrupt processes.

Related systems extend staged agent assistance into adjacent settings. PaperDebugger[^4] presents a plugin-based multi-agent assistant for in-editor academic writing, review, and editing, while PaperQA[^5] provides a modular retrieval-augmented research agent with separate main and search agents. These systems establish practical patterns for editor integration and delegated research. The survey does not describe either system as an executable mapping of Flower and Hayes' Monitor[^1], goal network, and recursive process organization.

The contrast is therefore about process control rather than the presence of multiple agents. STORM[^3] and related systems can separate research, planning, drafting, and polishing into effective stages, whereas the present plugin records why a Monitor selects Planning, Translating, or Reviewing and permits Generate and Evaluate to interrupt another process. The [pinned protocol](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/fe09dd03b379ae818df3db8acc9555b7b3f201f5/docs/experiments/protocol.md) compares that recursive condition with single-shot, linear-stage, STORM-style, no-goal-network, and fixed-order conditions under a common information policy.

## Human-AI co-writing and writing-support tools

CoAuthor[^6] establishes a process-oriented dataset for human-AI collaborative writing. The dataset records writer prompts, model continuations, acceptance behavior, and revisions, which makes interaction visible beyond the final document. CoAuthor is therefore a useful precedent for studying how people work with language models, although its central object is the collaboration trace rather than a theory-derived controller for the writing process.

The In2Writing design-space work uses cognitive process theory to organize writing-support research. Gero et al.[^7] define a design space around the part of writing supported by a tool and the constraints on the supported writing goal, drawing on the non-linear, goal-directed model of Flower and Hayes[^1]. Their review of writing-support work identifies gaps in which process areas and goal constraints have received attention. The design-space paper uses the theory as a taxonomy for comparing tools; our plugin uses the theory to specify executable roles, transitions, goal files, and trace events.

The wider In2Writing series supplies complementary process precedents. Schneider et al. relate data-to-text pipeline architecture to research on human writing processes and argue that process-level optimization matters when evaluating every generated output is impractical[^8]. Du et al.'s Read, Revise, Repeat system lets a model propose edits, lets writers accept or reject them, and feeds accepted edits into later iterations[^9]. Liu and August[^10] ground an intelligent writing tool in writing-center tutoring practices, while Kim et al.[^11] study conversational interaction as a setting for reflection and substantive revision. These systems inform human control and revision analysis, but they do not provide the specific theory-to-plugin operationalization studied here.

## Writing-quality evaluation

The evaluation design combines broad, long-text, and structured writing benchmarks. WritingBench[^12] supplies general-purpose writing queries with instance-specific criteria, HelloBench[^13] targets long-text generation across several task types, and DoLoMiTes[^14] supplies methodical expert-authored tasks suited to reports and design documents. The [pinned evaluation survey](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/03f0f1c981a131f11a439a932ebfe839532046bc/docs/research/writing-eval-datasets.md) selects these three as the primary set and treats argumentative corpora such as PERSUADE 2.0[^16] and ICLE++[^17] as prompt and calibration resources rather than direct substitutes for model-quality labels.

Judge methodology must account for both rubric variation and pairwise presentation effects. The protocol assigns two judge families, uses a five-dimension pointwise rubric with evidence quotes, and evaluates every condition pair in both A/B orders with blinded labels; the open evaluator follows the Prometheus 2 design[^15]. Human validation samples pairwise judgments with three annotators per comparison. The protocol freezes these settings before scoring and reports product quality separately from trace-derived measures.

Process evaluation complements final-output evaluation in this setting. The protocol analyzes goal quantity and specificity, middle-range goal quantity and quality, goal regeneration, process-switch transitions, interruptions, pop-back events, and revision intent, and it relates those measures to per-prompt quality. The trace is an operational analogue of a thinking-aloud protocol, not a direct transcript of private reasoning, so missing or ambiguous events remain data-quality findings rather than inferred cognition.

## Positioning

Our work occupies the gap between theory-informed writing-support taxonomies and effective but staged LLM writing pipelines. The [survey gap analysis](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/371b33bf5c1ac4c4ec89c6bfa9f0648c490af094/docs/research/skill-subagent-survey.md) found no surveyed system that maps Flower and Hayes' roles[^1] onto an installable plugin. The [pinned plugin artifact](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/af39bb164bc352b53e93bcf9c91fe8c715bd0cd3/plugin/README.md) turns that bounded gap into a cross-platform implementation with externalized goals and an append-only process trace, while the [pinned protocol](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/fe09dd03b379ae818df3db8acc9555b7b3f201f5/docs/experiments/protocol.md) tests its product and process claims under controlled comparisons.
