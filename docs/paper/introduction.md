# Introduction

Long-form large language model (LLM) generation is often organized as a fixed sequence of stages. The sequence may take the form of Pre-Write, Write, and Re-Write, or a research-oriented chain of perspective discovery, question answering, outlining, drafting, and polishing, as in the STORM system[^2]. That organization separates responsibilities cleanly, but it also resembles the stage model that Flower and Hayes rejected as an account of how human writers compose[^1]. A system can therefore produce a polished document while leaving the decisions that connect planning, drafting, and revision outside the process being modeled.

Flower and Hayes' Cognitive Process Theory of Writing[^1] treats composing as a goal-directed organization of thinking processes rather than a sequence of completed stages. Planning generates and organizes ideas and sets goals, Translating turns represented meanings into written language, and Reviewing evaluates and revises text or plans. A Monitor determines when to invoke these processes, which may be embedded within one another, while a hierarchical network of goals gives the activity direction. Writers may also regenerate higher-level goals as the developing text and new understanding change the rhetorical problem.

We operationalize that account as an installable writing plugin for Claude Code and OpenAI Codex. The plugin maps the Monitor to the main skill and maps Planning, Translating, and Reviewing to delegated role skills and adapters; the mapping is an implementation interpretation of the theory, not a claim that the 1981 paper specifies software files. The plugin externalizes the assignment, goals, draft, memory, and plans in project files, and it appends process switches, decisions, evidence, and unresolved questions to a JSON Lines trace. The [pinned plugin README](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/af39bb164bc352b53e93bcf9c91fe8c715bd0cd3/plugin/README.md) documents the Claude Code and Codex installation paths and the file-backed state.

The experiment tests process organization under equal information and fixed resource policies. The [pinned experiment protocol](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/fe09dd03b379ae818df3db8acc9555b7b3f201f5/docs/experiments/protocol.md) settles four research questions:

1. **RQ1.** Does the theory-based recursive Monitor and goal-network architecture produce better writing than a single-shot system and linear-stage pipelines? A goal network is a hierarchy of writing goals that the Monitor coordinates during drafting.
2. **RQ2.** Which components matter? We compare the full plugin with ablation conditions A5 and A6, which remove or constrain one component of the full design.
3. **RQ3.** Do agent traces, analyzed as thinking-aloud protocols that record observable writing actions, show the goal creation and regeneration dynamics described by Flower and Hayes[^1]? The RQ3 analysis includes the prediction that the quantity and quality of middle-range goals, which connect broad rhetorical aims to local writing actions, relate to writing quality.
4. **RQ4.** Does the mapping replicate across platforms?

The paper makes four contributions. First, it gives an executable operationalization of a cognitive writing-process theory in which process selection, goal changes, and revision can be inspected. Second, it packages the same process mapping for Claude Code and Codex through host-specific manifests and adapters. Third, it treats externalized project state and an append-only decision trace as objects of process analysis, alongside final text quality. Fourth, among the systems covered by the [platform and skill survey](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing-process/blob/371b33bf5c1ac4c4ec89c6bfa9f0648c490af094/docs/research/skill-subagent-survey.md), we found no system that maps Flower and Hayes' roles[^1] onto an installable plugin; this is a survey-scoped gap statement, not an exhaustive claim about all writing software.
