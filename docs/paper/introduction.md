# Introduction

Long-form large language model (LLM) writing systems motivate a controlled comparison between fixed-stage agent pipelines and recursive process organization. The [`docs/experiments/protocol.md`](../../docs/experiments/protocol.md) instantiates two fixed-stage baselines. A2 passes through Pre-Write, Write, and Re-Write, whereas A3 uses a STORM-style sequence of perspective discovery, simulated question answering, outlining, drafting, and polishing without retrieval under the equal-information policy. The comparison uses STORM[^2] as the research-to-draft reference for this stage sequence, while Flower and Hayes' theory[^1] was formulated in response to stage models of human composing. The comparison tests whether this difference in process organization is associated with output quality and observable process behavior; it does not treat either baseline as a universal description of LLM writing.

Flower and Hayes' Cognitive Process Theory of Writing[^1] treats composing as a goal-directed organization of thinking processes rather than a sequence of completed stages. Planning generates and organizes ideas and sets goals, Translating turns represented meanings into written language, and Reviewing evaluates and revises text or plans. A Monitor coordinates these processes, which may be embedded within one another, while a hierarchical network of goals connects broad rhetorical aims to more operational sub-goals. Writers may regenerate higher-level goals as the developing text and new understanding change the rhetorical problem.

Our approach is a theory-informed, installable writing plugin for Claude Code and OpenAI Codex. The [`plugin/README.md`](../../plugin/README.md) documents the installation paths and platform adapters, while the repository [`README.md`](../../README.md) documents the mapping from the Monitor, Planning, Translating, and Reviewing to the shipped artifacts. The plugin externalizes the assignment, goals, draft, memory, and plans in project files and maintains an append-only JSON Lines decision trace containing process switches, decisions, evidence, and unresolved questions. The mapping is an implementation interpretation of the theory, not a claim that the 1981 paper specifies software files.

The experiment evaluates process organization under equal information and fixed resource policies. The [`docs/experiments/protocol.md`](../../docs/experiments/protocol.md) defines a goal network as a hierarchy of writing goals that the Monitor coordinates during drafting and poses four research questions:

1. **RQ1.** Does the theory-based recursive Monitor and goal-network architecture produce better writing than a single-shot system and linear-stage pipelines?
2. **RQ2.** Which components matter? We compare the full plugin with ablation conditions A5 and A6, which remove or constrain one component of the full design.
3. **RQ3.** Do agent traces, treated as an operational analogue of a thinking-aloud protocol that records observable writing actions rather than private cognition, show the goal creation and regeneration dynamics described by Flower and Hayes[^1]? Flower and Hayes predict that "an important difference between good and poor writers will be in both the quantity and quality of the middle range of goals they create."[^1] The analysis tests whether these goals, which connect broad rhetorical aims to local writing actions, relate to writing quality.
4. **RQ4.** Does the mapping replicate across platforms?

The paper makes four contributions. First, it gives an executable operationalization of a cognitive writing-process theory in which process selection, goal changes, and revision can be inspected. Second, it packages the same process mapping for Claude Code and Codex through host-specific manifests and adapters. Third, it treats externalized project state and an append-only decision trace as objects of process analysis alongside final text quality. Fourth, among the systems covered by [`docs/research/skill-subagent-survey.md`](../../docs/research/skill-subagent-survey.md), the survey reports no system that maps Flower and Hayes' roles[^1] onto an installable plugin; this is a survey-scoped gap statement, not an exhaustive claim about all writing software.
