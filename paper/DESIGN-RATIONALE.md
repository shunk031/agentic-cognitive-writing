# Design rationale

This entry point records only reasons stated in the repository's research and protocol documents or subsequently supplied by the author. Where those sources record a setting without a reason, it says so.

- **Primary benchmarks.** The study uses WritingBench (Apache-2.0) for general-purpose writing, HelloBench (MIT) for long-text generation, and DoLoMiTes (Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0 materials) for structured expert writing. The survey records one benchmark for each writing kind, clear license signals, and a product-level evaluation path: WritingBench supplies query-specific criteria, HelloBench supplies long-text checklist evaluation, and DoLoMiTes supplies methodical expert tasks. The protocol fixes these three as the primary set. See [`writing-eval-datasets.md`](../docs/research/writing-eval-datasets.md) and [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Excluded candidates.** PERSUADE 2.0 and ICLE++ are prompt sources or human-score anchors, not primary benchmarks. LongBench-Write remains optional because benchmark-file licensing and provenance are unresolved. EQ-Bench remains a secondary creative probe because its license is unresolved; WildBench remains a broad-assistant subset rather than a pure writing benchmark; WritingPreferenceBench is a judge-sensitivity reference rather than a generation benchmark and has conflicting license metadata. LongGenBench is a supplementary long-context robustness probe, and LongJudgeBench validates judges rather than generated writing. ASAP is a prompt source and historical scoring reference with access terms to check. These roles and exclusions are recorded in [`writing-eval-datasets.md`](../docs/research/writing-eval-datasets.md) and [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **DoLoMiTes split.** The study uses only the development subset. The prompt README records an archive-derived 820-dev/1,037-test split, says that the archive count is authoritative over the upstream README's 830-dev statement, and keeps the test subset out of primary analysis. The protocol gives no further reason for this development-only choice. See [`prompts/README.md`](../experiments/prompts/README.md) and [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Hard prompt slice.** Agents built on current frontier models already handle routine writing prompts, so the study evaluates the prompts that demand the most. The selection key is prompt word count plus the largest output length the prompt or its constraints request; prompts above 6,000 words in either part are excluded, and the 100 highest-scoring prompts per benchmark form the 300-prompt hard set. What makes a prompt hard differs by benchmark, so the manuscript should say "long inputs and, where stated, long requested outputs" rather than "long outputs" throughout. Medians computed from the committed manifests with the selection code:

  | Benchmark | Pool size | Pool median prompt words | Hard-set median prompt words | Hard prompts stating a length | Hard-set median requested length |
  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
  | WritingBench | 1,000 | 288 | 570 | 100% | 5,000 |
  | HelloBench | 647 | 84 | 3,915 | 4% | none stated |
  | DoLoMiTes | 820 | 316 | 582 | 27% | none stated |

  HelloBench hard prompts are long-input tasks (82 summarization, 14 text completion, 4 chat); WritingBench hard prompts are both long-input and long-output. See [`prompts/README.md`](../experiments/prompts/README.md) and [`prompt_sets.py`](../experiments/src/agentic_cogwriter/prompts/prompt_sets.py).
- **Why 100 prompts per benchmark suffice.** The sufficiency claim is a power statement sourced in `numbers.tex` through the `\Power*` macros. Pooled over the three benchmarks, a contrast leaves about 282 eligible pairs and 220 decided comparisons, enough for the exact sign test at the first Holm threshold (0.05/24) to detect a true preference of about 63 percent with 80 percent power; a 100-prompt benchmark cell, with about 75 decided comparisons, detects preferences near 73 percent. The observed pooled preferences for Agentic CogWriter over Single-pass, Staged, and Task-planning (80.9, 67.3, and 68.5 percent) all exceed the pooled threshold, and the cell threshold explains why Holm support is near-complete against Single-pass (8 of 9 cells) and partial against Staged and Task-planning (3 and 4 of 9). The full benchmark pools were generated once but never judged, so the manuscript makes no claim about them.

- **Estimands after the pilot.** A ten-prompt WritingBench pilot showed that the generic five-dimension scale saturated while benchmark-native criteria and pairwise comparisons discriminated among conditions. The protocol therefore assigns native and generic pointwise scores as descriptive outputs and pairwise preference as the confirmatory estimand; the session handoff records the pilot decision. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md) and [issue 32](https://github.com/shunk031/agentic-cognitive-writing/issues/32).
- **Information and retrieval.** Every condition receives the same assignment, context, tools, context-window policy, timeout, output budget, and allowed attempts. The no-retrieval rule forbids web search, network retrieval, external browsing, and unprovided sources. The protocol records this as the equal-information policy and gives no separate reason beyond holding supplied information constant. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Seven pre-specified conditions.** A1 is single-shot generation; A2 is the fixed Pre-Write/Write/Re-Write pipeline; A3 is an adaptive task-planning, WriteHERE-style baseline without retrieval; A4 is the proposed Agentic CogWriter plugin; A5 removes the explicit goal network; A6 retains the state but fixes process order; and A7 folds the work into one writer. The protocol records the provenance and behavior of each baseline. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Post-hoc single-context check.** A8 was added only after the primary results had been observed, to test whether the A7 `Single-writer` loss could be attributed to separate role-agent contexts. It keeps A4's Monitor, process instructions, shared draft, goal network, history, trace and termination contracts, but executes the selected processes in the host agent context without subagents. The analysis reading was recorded before judging, and the condition remains outside the locked confirmatory family. The manuscript therefore uses A8 to constrain attribution, not to promote a new confirmatory mechanism claim. See PR #77 discussion and the corresponding protocol addendum.
- **Judge family.** The reported pairwise study uses `gpt-5.6-sol` to judge `gpt-5.6-luna` generations, so the primary judge and generator belong to the same GPT-5.6 family. This does not satisfy the separately pre-specified plan to use a different-family judge. The manuscript must disclose that deviation and treat the `claude-sonnet-5` re-evaluation as a cross-family robustness check rather than averaging the two judges. This matters because prior work documents evaluator position, verbosity, and self/family-preference biases. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md#same-family-confirmatory-judging-2026-09-29).
- **Coding-agent harness.** The system packages the Monitor and role skills as a plugin with Planner, Translator, and Reviewer subagents, and the protocol maps those roles to the three writing processes. The ablations change process instructions or the Monitor's selection rule while keeping the shared framework, inputs, and tools fixed; the protocol records this as the way to test the account as an agent process. See [`skill-subagent-survey.md`](../docs/research/skill-subagent-survey.md) and [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Confirmatory family.** The prompt-collapsed outcome gives one semantic winner when both presentation orders agree and a tie for any disagreement, including winner versus tie. Ties leave the exact two-sided sign test, and Holm correction covers the 24 cells formed by eight locked contrasts across three replications for each benchmark. The protocol records these rules as the confirmatory estimand and family definition. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Replications.** The protocol defines three independent runs for the 24-cell family and keeps the replication analysis separate from primary scoring. The repository records the count and separation rule but gives no additional reason for choosing three. See [`protocol.md`](../docs/experiments/protocol.md).
- **Generator and judge models.** Every condition generates with `gpt-5.6-luna` at medium reasoning effort through the Codex CLI, and all reported judgments use `gpt-5.6-sol` at medium effort; the cross-family check uses `claude-sonnet-5` at medium effort. The author selected Luna for generation because the experiment requires high-volume long-form generation across many systems, prompts, and replications, and OpenAI describes Luna as optimized for cost-sensitive, high-volume workloads and as the fastest, lowest-cost GPT-5.6 model. Sol was selected for evaluation because OpenAI positions it as the GPT-5.6 flagship for complex professional work and reports stronger performance than Luna across multiple professional and reasoning benchmarks. The intended role split is therefore a cost-efficient generator evaluated by a higher-capability model. See the official [`GPT-5.6 Luna`](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-luna), [`GPT-5.6 Sol`](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-sol), and [`GPT-5.6 release`](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/) pages. The repository does not record a separate rationale for choosing medium reasoning effort.
- **Benchmark-native scores.** These are each benchmark's own LLM-as-a-judge procedure, run with `gpt-5.6-sol` instead of the original papers' judge models. WritingBench: the judge scores each of the prompt's five query-specific criteria from 1 to 10 with the upstream critic prompt, and the native score is their mean ([`writingbench-native-v1.md`](../experiments/prompts/judges/writingbench-native-v1.md)). HelloBench: the judge answers the prompt's 5 to 7 yes-or-no checklist items on the upstream scale 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, or 1, and the native score is their mean ([`hellobench-native-v1.md`](../experiments/prompts/judges/hellobench-native-v1.md)). DoLoMiTes ships no per-prompt criteria and has no native score. Because the judge model differs, the values are not comparable with published leaderboard numbers; a footnote suffices for that caveat.
- **Generic pointwise composite.** The judge scores each output from 1 to 5 on five protocol-defined dimensions ([`pointwise-v1.md`](../experiments/prompts/judges/pointwise-v1.md), adapted from the FastChat single-answer prompt). Each dimension is z-scored within a benchmark across all conditions' outputs, and the five z-scores are averaged. The composite is relative: 0 is the average output of all conditions on that benchmark, and a negative value means below that average, not a poor absolute score. The study adopted it because the pilot showed the raw 1-to-5 scale saturating.
- **Pairwise judgment.** The judge sees the assignment and two blinded outputs and returns one of them or a tie ([`pairwise-v1.md`](../experiments/prompts/judges/pairwise-v1.md), adapted from the FastChat pairwise prompt with its position-bias and length-bias instructions). Each pair is judged twice with the output order swapped because LLM judges favor a position (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024); the two verdicts become one outcome per prompt, a win only when both orders agree and a tie otherwise. Pairwise preference carries the confirmatory tests because the pilot showed pointwise scales compressing the differences among conditions while pairwise comparison separated them.
- **Robustness checks.** Two documented judge biases motivate them. LLM judges prefer longer outputs (Zheng et al., 2023; Dubois et al., 2024), which the output-length and compute-matched sensitivity analyses address using the existing judgments; and LLM judges favor outputs from their own model family (Panickssery et al., 2024), which the cross-family judge addresses on three contrasts.
- **Rare looping is not explained by easy prompts.** A reviewer may argue that the `Monitor` rarely returns to an earlier process because the prompts are easy. Three analyses of existing artifacts, with no new model calls, answer this. Within the hard set (886 completed Full runs over three replications), returns from `Reviewing` per run are 0.158 in the easiest third and 0.163 in the hardest third, runs with more than one pass are 1.0 and 0.3 percent, and the Spearman correlations of the difficulty key with returns and with the multi-pass indicator are 0.058 (p = 0.08) and -0.014 (p = 0.67). Across the full benchmark pools (2,440 completed Full runs generated once with identical generator settings and never judged), where difficulty spans single-word prompts to 100,000-word requests, the correlations stay near zero (returns 0.014, p = 0.49; multi-pass 0.011, p = 0.58), returns are 0.191 in the easiest third and 0.193 in the hardest, and only switches show a negligible association with requested length (0.050, p = 0.013). The hard prompts are not trivially solved: in the hardest third, Full reaches 90 percent of the requested length in 59 percent of runs against 86 percent in the easiest third, the judge decides 80 percent of Full-versus-Single-pass prompts, and WritingBench native means (7.04 for Single-pass, 7.54 for Full on 1 to 10) sit below the ceiling. The numbers come from an analysis JSON kept under the ignored `paper/scratch/loop-difficulty/`; they need macros through `scripts/make_numbers.py` before they enter the manuscript.
- **Earlier formal method and comparison tables.** The formal method that defines the control state (D_t, G_t, H_t) and the decision a_t = pi_monitor(D_t, G_t, H_t), the decision-equation comparison table, and the check-mark mechanism table are in this branch's history before commit 20bd9ac (`git show 20bd9ac^:paper/sec/03_method.tex`, `paper/tab/architecture-comparison.tex`, `paper/tab/appendix-comparison.tex`).
