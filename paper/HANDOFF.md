# Paper handoff

This note records the public-safe standing rules for the manuscript in this directory.

## Manuscript decisions

- The pre-specified study compares seven writers on demanding subsets from WritingBench, HelloBench, and DoLoMiTes. The selector prioritizes the longest inputs and, where stated, the longest requested outputs; the manuscript also reports a post-hoc exploratory `Single-context` condition used only to test the attribution of the `Single-writer` gap.
- The study uses three generation runs that re-generate the same prompt set with the same stack. They are repeated stochastic generations, not independent replications of the claim. The confirmatory outcome combines the two presentation orders into one prompt-level outcome, excludes ties from the exact sign test, and applies Holm correction to the locked benchmark-by-contrast family. The `Single-context` check sits outside that confirmatory family.
- Headline pairwise win rates condition on decided comparisons: both presentation orders must agree on a winner, while ties and pairs with a failed output are excluded. The manuscript also reports unconditional attempted-prompt shares and a worst-case missing-output sensitivity for the headline architecture contrasts.
- For `Fixed-order` and `No-goals`, the pre-specified benchmark-by-generation-run cells have no Holm-surviving difference. A separate post-hoc prompt-clustered analysis pooled over benchmarks and the three generation runs finds a small preference for adaptive ordering and no significant pooled goal-network effect. This analysis is exploratory and constrains both components to modest contributions rather than establishing equivalence or irrelevance.
- The manuscript distinguishes the `Monitor`, `Planner`, `Translator`, and `Reviewer` agents from the `Planning`, `Translating`, and `Reviewing` processes.
- The coding-agent harness, the instruction-based ablations, and the three benchmark sources are documented in the manuscript and in the repository's experiment protocol.

## Writing rules

These rules come from the author's reviews of earlier drafts; each names a defect class that recurred.

- **Targeted feedback stays targeted.** When the author points to a specific sentence, phrase, paragraph defect, or enumerated set of issues, edit only the text needed to resolve those items. Do not rewrite neighboring sentences, restructure the paragraph, add new results, change the argument order, or otherwise "improve" surrounding prose unless the author explicitly asks for broader revision. If resolving an item genuinely requires changing adjacent text, show the exact before/after wording and the reason before applying that broader change. After the edit, compare the manuscript against the HEAD from immediately before the feedback batch and inspect every prose change; any changed sentence that cannot be mapped one-to-one to an author instruction must be reverted. This rule takes precedence over stylistic cleanup opportunities discovered while editing.
- **Model the venue.** Follow how published ACL and NAACL long-form generation and agent papers phrase each section, and introduce no term those papers do not use. Undefined coinages that confused readers and must not return: "prompt-collapsed outcome", "product quality" or "product-level", "controlled system", "host agent", "component intervention", "matched-output comparison", "realized sequence". Use "ablation", "document quality", and a plain description of the two-order judgment instead.
- **Paragraph writing.** Every body paragraph opens with a topic sentence that states its point and continues with support. A one-sentence paragraph is a defect, and `tools/check-markdown.py` fails on one. A paragraph that starts after a break cannot open with "this" or "these" referring to the previous paragraph.
- **Results paragraphs open with their evidence.** A Results paragraph opens with the figure or table it discusses ("Table 3 shows ..."), states the finding in that sentence, and supports it after. The Discussion cites the same figures and tables.
- **Citations.** Use `\citet` for textual citations so the output reads "Flower and Hayes (1981)", and `\citep` for parenthetical ones. A named theory, system, or benchmark carries its citation at first mention, as in "The cognitive process theory of writing~\citep{flower1981cognitive}". The first sentence of each Introduction paragraph cites the line of work it summarizes, and broad claims cite several papers across the fields involved. Match the citation density of recent ACL and NAACL long-form generation papers; the manuscript currently cites 30 distinct works.
- **Prior-work grouping.** Do not write a plural catch-all such as "others" when only one citation supports it. When describing adaptive structural reasoning during generation, cite representative systems explicitly; the current Introduction uses CogWriter and IS-CoT together. Describe what the cited systems actually do before drawing the process-level contrast.
- **Agent context.** Use "Recent coding agents" when introducing the contemporary coding-agent trend. Do not coordinate `coding agents` with the broader category `AI agents` as if they were parallel classes. Beyond coding, describe agentic knowledge-work workflows by the outputs they produce rather than narrowing the motivation to "autonomous research agents".
- **Monitor capitalization.** `Monitor` is capitalized only for the named Agentic CogWriter agent role, as in `Monitor agent`. Flower and Hayes's generic control construct is `monitor` in lowercase. `tools/check-introduction-style.py` enforces this distinction.
- **Datasets by purpose.** Wherever the benchmarks appear, including the abstract and introduction, say what kind of writing each one tests and why, before or instead of the bare names: "To evaluate X, we use WritingBench~\citep{...}, which covers ...". Describe the hard slice with the facts in [`DESIGN-RATIONALE.md`](DESIGN-RATIONALE.md), using "longest inputs and, where stated, longest requested outputs" rather than "longest-output slice".
- **Abstract reader flow.** Earlier revisions titled `paper: tighten abstract around reader-facing contribution` and `ci: lock introduction and abstract reader-facing structure` were intended to make the paper readable from front to back, but the latter only locked the proposal structure in the Introduction. A later rewrite could therefore restore an abrupt `Agentic CogWriter instead ...` opening. The Abstract must proceed from contemporary agent context to the writing-control problem, then introduce the contribution as `we propose Agentic CogWriter`, then evaluation and results. Do not name Agentic CogWriter before the proposal sentence, and avoid ambiguous references such as `the system` when the system name is clearer.
- **Abstract evidence level.** Keep prompt-selection mechanics such as `subset` out of the Abstract; they belong in Experimental Design and Limitations. Do not summarize pointwise results by saying `two` benchmark types: that wording makes the reader immediately ask what happened on the remaining benchmark. Present pointwise and pairwise evaluation symmetrically. When ablations do not isolate one component, frame them as supporting a system-level interpretation rather than foregrounding a weak exploratory effect. A trace sentence must explain that adaptive departures are used selectively rather than introducing undefined shorthand such as `observed writing trajectories`.
- **Introduction evidence framing.** State the experimental control directly (`we hold ... constant across the compared conditions`) rather than referring to an undefined `Every system`. Name both pointwise rubric scoring and pairwise same-prompt preference evaluation. The Introduction should lead with the clear system-level preference result, then use ablations and traces to refine its interpretation: the gain is not attributed to one tested component, and adaptive ordering is available but used selectively. Keep confirmatory/exploratory statistical qualification in Results rather than making the Introduction read like a self-rejection.
- **Proposal and method.** The abstract and introduction use "we propose Agentic CogWriter". In the Introduction, introduce the system and its definition in one sentence rather than repeating `Agentic CogWriter` in the following sentence. The method section is titled "Agentic CogWriter", opens by introducing the system and pointing to Figure 1, and defines the inputs, outputs, state, and each module formally; the earlier formulation is recorded in [`DESIGN-RATIONALE.md`](DESIGN-RATIONALE.md).
- **Figures and tables.** Figure 1 sits at the top of the right column of page 1 and is cited from the Introduction. Every figure is rendered and inspected after each change. Result tables mark the best score in each column in bold and say "Best scores in bold." in the caption. Narrow tables fit one column; a multi-panel figure uses one PDF per panel arranged with minipages.
- **Placement.** Asides such as the non-comparability of native scores with published numbers go in footnotes, not body sentences. Appendix references name a top-level appendix ("Appendix E"); each appendix topic gets its own top-level appendix section. The main text never cites a table or figure that appears only in the appendix; it cites the appendix section, and `tools/check-markdown.py` fails otherwise. Avoid "primary" for the pairwise evaluation; motivate pointwise and pairwise evaluation in their own paragraphs instead.

## Build and review rules

- Numbers in the manuscript come from `scripts/make_numbers.py` and the recorded analysis inputs.
- Every source change requires the PDF and Markdown builds, the Markdown checker, and rendering of changed pages with `tools/render-pages.sh`.
- A successful build has zero diagnostics, defined references and citations, and consistent symbol, name, and notation ledgers.
- Public artifacts report tokens and timings where they are study results. They do not publish private operational details or derived prices.
- The human-evaluation packet is retained as a deferred study artifact; raw response passages are not part of the public manuscript commit.
