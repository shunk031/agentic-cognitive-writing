The comparison is deliberately within our controlled evaluation rather than against published leaderboard values. \condStaged\ and \condTaskPlanning\ instantiate organizations used in prior long-form writing systems, but all rows use our common generator, coding-agent harness, selected prompt slice, and output contract. \subsection{Pairwise preference (RQ1)}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

Under same-family judging, \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to all tested baseline organizations: \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports descriptive across-replication summaries of the primary prompt-collapsed outcome. \condAgenticCogWriter{}'s mean win rate across replications is \PooledFourOneRateMean\ against \condSinglePass, \PooledFourTwoRateMean\ against \condStaged, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean\ against \condTaskPlanning\ after pooling benchmark outcomes within each replication. The direction is stable across benchmarks and replications: \condAgenticCogWriter\ is favored in every benchmark-by-replication cell against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, with only a single exception against \condStaged. After Holm correction within each benchmark's locked family, \PooledFourOneHolmCellsTotal, \PooledFourTwoHolmCellsTotal, and \PooledFourThreeHolmCellsTotal\ benchmark-by-replication cells survive for the respective contrasts. These counts refer to the replicate-specific confirmatory tests, not to tests of the displayed across-replication means. Together, the stable directions and replicate-specific tests provide evidence of a performance difference between the tested organizations; they do not isolate which shared feature of \condAgenticCogWriter\ produces it. Replicate-level estimates and intervals are shown in Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise-detail}.

\subsection{Ablations (RQ2)}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

The ablations give a selective answer. Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} reports descriptive across-replication summaries for the two confirmatory single-factor interventions and the bundled diagnostic intervention, all in the same direction as \condAgenticCogWriter{}'s win rate. Removing the explicit goal representation leaves the pairwise result close to even, and no benchmark-by-replication cell survives Holm correction. Replacing adaptive process choice with the fixed process cycle likewise produces no surviving benchmark-by-replication cell. These contrasts do not establish equivalence; under the confirmatory design, neither intervention produces a detectable loss.

\input{tab/ablation-results}

The diagnostic \condSingleWriter\ condition performs substantially worse, but it is not a clean delegation or role-context ablation. It retains adaptive process choice and the goal network while executing all processes in one writer context and restricting each \texttt{Translating} step to one paragraph before the next process decision. The contrast therefore changes delegation, control granularity, and output length together. The narrower exploratory \condSingleContext\ intervention retains the process loop while executing each selected process in the host agent context. It wins \SingleContextFullRate\ of non-tied comparisons against \condAgenticCogWriter\ ($p=\SingleContextFullP$, unadjusted), showing no detectable difference after pooling benchmark outcomes within that exploratory replication. This check also differs in gateway entry point and transport from the pre-specified runs, so it is not a clean context-only intervention. Because it was added after the primary results and sits outside the confirmatory family, the result only constrains a simple attribution to separated role contexts; it neither establishes equivalence nor identifies an alternative mechanism. Appendix~\ref{app:single-context} reports the full exploratory check.

\subsection{Agent behavior (RQ3)}
\label{sec:results-process-dynamics}

The agent traces reveal a second part of the story that product scores alone cannot show. Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics} summarizes observable controller behavior. The \texttt{Monitor} usually follows the \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle, rarely returns from \texttt{Reviewing} to \texttt{Planning}, and rarely accepts goal regeneration. Thus, the realized schedule is usually close to a regular process cycle, while accepted replacement of existing goals through regeneration is rare.

The traces help interpret the interventions but do not map onto them one-to-one. The prevalence of the canonical process cycle directly contextualizes the \condFixedOrder\ result: unrestricted scheduling often realizes an order close to the imposed cycle. Rare goal regeneration shows only that accepted replacement of existing goals is uncommon. It does not show that the explicit goal representation is unused: goals can still be created, developed, and consulted during later process decisions and drafting.

Table~\ref{tab:trace-outcome} connects realized process sequences to pairwise outcomes descriptively. The preference rate is similar whether \condAgenticCogWriter\ follows exactly one \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle or another sequence, both against \condFixedOrder\ and against \condSingleWriter. Thus, in these comparisons, departures from the canonical cycle are not associated with a larger pairwise advantage.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The main RQ1 direction survives the tested output-length and compute restrictions, but judge robustness remains limited. The same-family judge prefers the longer output in \LengthLongerRate\ of non-tied pairs, yet \condAgenticCogWriter\ remains favored over \condSinglePass\ within the tested output-length matching bands. Within the tightest compute-matching band reported in Appendix~\ref{app:compute-control}, \condAgenticCogWriter\ remains preferred to \condStaged\ at \ComputeFullStagedWithinOneTwentyFiveRate\ and to \condTaskPlanning\ at \ComputeFullTaskPlanningWithinOneTwentyFiveRate; there are no \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ pairs in that band. These realized-compute analyses are descriptive rather than causal because compute is itself an outcome of execution.

A cross-family judge preserves the across-benchmark pooled direction for the checked contrasts, including \CrossFamilyFullSinglePassWinRate\ for \condAgenticCogWriter\ versus \condSinglePass\ and \CrossFamilyFullTaskPlanningWinRate\ versus \condTaskPlanning, but yields a non-tied prompt-collapsed judgment on only \CrossFamilyPooledCommitRate\ of eligible pairs and reverses \condAgenticCogWriter\ versus \condSinglePass\ on WritingBench. We therefore treat it as a robustness check rather than replacement confirmatory evidence. Length, compute, and cross-family sensitivity analyses appear in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses}.
