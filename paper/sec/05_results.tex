\subsection{Pairwise preference (RQ1)}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

Under same-family judging, \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to all tested baseline organizations. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports descriptive across-replication summaries of the prompt-collapsed outcome. Its across-benchmark mean win rate is \PooledFourOneRateMean\ against \condSinglePass, \PooledFourTwoRateMean\ against \condStaged, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean\ against \condTaskPlanning. The direction is stable across benchmarks and replications: \condAgenticCogWriter\ is favored in every benchmark-by-replication cell against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, with only one exception against \condStaged. Replicate-specific sign tests with within-benchmark Holm correction support this pattern; the full intervals and cell-level decisions are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise-detail}. Together, these results provide evidence of a performance difference between the tested organizations without isolating which shared feature of \condAgenticCogWriter\ produces it.

\subsection{Ablations (RQ2)}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} reports descriptive across-replication summaries for the two confirmatory single-factor interventions and the bundled diagnostic intervention. Removing the explicit goal representation leaves the pairwise result close to even, and no benchmark-by-replication cell survives Holm correction. Replacing adaptive process choice with the fixed process cycle yields the same inferential pattern. These contrasts do not establish equivalence; under the confirmatory design, neither intervention produces a detectable loss.

\input{tab/ablation-results}

The diagnostic \condSingleWriter\ condition performs substantially worse, but it is not a clean delegation or role-context ablation: it also changes control granularity and output length. The narrower post-hoc \condSingleContext\ check retains the process loop and shows no detectable difference from \condAgenticCogWriter\ after pooling benchmark outcomes within that exploratory replication (\SingleContextFullRate\ win rate for \condSingleContext, $p=\SingleContextFullP$, unadjusted). Because this run also differs in gateway entry point and transport, it only constrains a simple attribution to separated role contexts; it does not identify an alternative mechanism. Appendix~\ref{app:single-context} reports the benchmark-level results and implementation details.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The RQ1 direction survives the tested output-length and compute restrictions where eligible comparisons are available. The same-family judge favors longer outputs overall, but \condAgenticCogWriter\ remains favored over \condSinglePass\ within the pre-specified length-matching bands and over \condStaged\ and \condTaskPlanning\ under the tightest available compute matching. No \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ pairs fall in that compute band. The cross-family judge preserves the pooled direction for the checked contrasts but returns ties frequently and reverses the WritingBench \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ direction. We therefore treat these analyses as descriptive robustness checks; full results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses}.
