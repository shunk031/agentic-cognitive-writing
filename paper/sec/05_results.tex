\subsection{Direct Comparison of Writing Systems}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports a system-level separation in direct same-prompt comparisons.
\condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning, with across-benchmark mean win rates of \PooledFourOneRateMean, \PooledFourTwoRateMean, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean, respectively, among order-consistent comparisons.
The direction holds in every benchmark and generation run against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, and in all but one benchmark--run test against \condStaged.

Under the pre-specified within-benchmark Holm correction, \PooledFourOneHolmCellsTotal, \PooledFourThreeHolmCellsTotal, and \PooledFourTwoHolmCellsTotal\ of the nine benchmark--run tests separate our system from \condSinglePass, \condTaskPlanning, and \condStaged, respectively.\footnote{See Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise-detail} for run-level intervals and corrected results.}
The preference also persists under more conservative accounting: counting every attempted prompt yields win shares of \UncondFullSinglePassWinShare, \UncondFullStagedWinShare, and \UncondFullTaskPlanningWinShare, and assigning every missing pair against our system still leaves win rates of at least \WorstCaseFullSinglePassRate, \WorstCaseFullStagedRate, and \WorstCaseFullTaskPlanningRate\ among order-consistent comparisons.\footnote{See Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses} for sensitivity analyses: resource-matched comparisons largely preserve the direction, whereas cross-family rescoring is less stable, including a WritingBench reversal against \condSinglePass.}
The direct-comparison result supports the tested system as a whole because our system differs from the alternatives in several coupled design choices, including persistent writing state, a process-level action space, and repeated return to the \texttt{Monitor}.

\subsection{Ablations and Execution Diagnostics}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} places the two pre-specified ablations much closer to \condAgenticCogWriter\ than the alternative writing systems in Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results}.

An exploratory prompt-level summary defined after the main results places our system slightly ahead of both ablations, at \ClusteredFullFixedOrderRate\ [\ClusteredFullFixedOrderInterval], $p=\ClusteredFullFixedOrderP$, against \condFixedOrder\ and \ClusteredFullNoGoalsRate\ [\ClusteredFullNoGoalsInterval], $p=\ClusteredFullNoGoalsP$, against \condNoGoals; the two intervals overlap, so the summary does not rank the components.

Execution changes produce a larger but less easily isolated difference.

\subsection{Observed Writing-Process Behavior}
\label{sec:results-process-dynamics}

Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics} shows a mostly simple writing loop after repeat collapsing and leading-\texttt{Planning} reconstruction: \AfourCycleCompliantRate\ of complete paths conform to the \texttt{Planning}--\texttt{Translating}--\texttt{Reviewing} cycle structure, while \AfourReviewingToTranslatingRate\ of runs return from \texttt{Reviewing} to \texttt{Translating}.
Full replanning (\AfourReviewingToPlanningRate) and goal replacement (\AfourRegenerationRate) are rare, so adaptivity is used mainly for targeted redrafting.

Figure~\ref{fig:process-sequences} shows the same concentration: the forward \texttt{Planning}--\texttt{Translating}--\texttt{Reviewing} progression is most frequent, so our system looks close to \condFixedOrder.
Because the demanding subsets increase input or requested output length or procedural burden without guaranteeing a need to replan, the traces show that adaptive control is rarely exercised on these subsets, not that repeated replanning is unnecessary for difficult writing.
