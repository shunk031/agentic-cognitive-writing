\subsection{RQ1: Does organizing generation around writing processes help?}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

Under same-family judging, Agentic CogWriter is preferred to all three tested baseline organizations: \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports the primary pairwise outcomes. Agentic CogWriter's pooled mean win rate across replications is \PooledFourOneRateMean\ against \condSinglePass, \PooledFourTwoRateMean\ against \condStaged, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean\ against \condTaskPlanning. The direction is stable across benchmarks and replications: Agentic CogWriter is favored in every benchmark-by-replication cell against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, and in all but one cell against \condStaged. After Holm correction, \PooledFourOneHolmCellsTotal, \PooledFourTwoHolmCellsTotal, and \PooledFourThreeHolmCellsTotal\ of the benchmark-by-replication cells survive for the three contrasts, respectively. These contrasts establish a performance difference between the tested organizations; by themselves, they do not isolate which shared feature of Agentic CogWriter produces it. \subsection{RQ2: Which design commitments explain the advantage?}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

The ablations give a selective answer. Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} separates the confirmatory single-factor interventions from the bundled diagnostic intervention and the later exploratory check. Removing the explicit goal representation leaves the pairwise result close to even, and no benchmark cell survives Holm correction. Replacing adaptive process choice with the fixed process cycle likewise produces no surviving benchmark cell. These contrasts do not establish equivalence; under the confirmatory design, neither intervention produces a detectable loss.

\input{tab/ablation-results}

The diagnostic \condSingleWriter\ condition performs substantially worse, but it is not a clean delegation or role-context ablation. It retains adaptive process choice and the goal network while executing all processes in one writer context and restricting each \texttt{Translating} step to one paragraph before the next process decision. The contrast therefore changes delegation, control granularity, and output length together. The narrower exploratory \condSingleContext\ intervention retains the process loop while executing each selected process in the host agent context. It wins \SingleContextFullRate\ of non-tied comparisons against Agentic CogWriter ($p=\SingleContextFullP$), producing no detectable pooled difference. Because \condSingleContext\ was added after the primary results and sits outside the confirmatory family, it constrains an attribution to separated role contexts but does not establish equivalence or identify an alternative mechanism. Appendix~\ref{app:single-context} reports the full exploratory check.

\subsection{RQ3: How does the Monitor use the available control flexibility?}
\label{sec:results-process-dynamics}

The agent traces reveal a second part of the story that product scores alone cannot show. Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics} summarizes observable controller behavior. The \texttt{Monitor} usually follows the \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle, rarely returns from \texttt{Reviewing} to \texttt{Planning}, and rarely accepts goal regeneration. Thus, the architecture exposes adaptive process scheduling and mutable goals, but the realized policy is usually close to a regular process cycle with a largely stable goal network.

\input{tab/process-dynamics}

These traces align with the ablations: the flexibility removed by \condFixedOrder\ and \condNoGoals\ is only weakly exercised by the unrestricted system under the tested prompts. They do not show that the unused flexibility is unnecessary in general; they characterize how the controller behaves in these runs. Detailed process counts, resource use, and sequence distributions appear in Appendix~\ref{app:process-behavior}.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The main RQ1 direction survives the tested output-length and compute restrictions, but judge robustness remains limited. The same-family judge prefers the longer output in \LengthLongerRate\ of non-tied pairs, yet Agentic CogWriter remains favored over \condSinglePass\ within the tested output-length matching bands. Within the tightest compute-matching band reported in Appendix~\ref{app:compute-control}, Agentic CogWriter remains preferred to \condStaged\ at \ComputeFullStagedWithinOneTwentyFiveRate\ and to \condTaskPlanning\ at \ComputeFullTaskPlanningWithinOneTwentyFiveRate; there are no Agentic CogWriter--\condSinglePass\ pairs in that band. These realized-compute analyses are descriptive rather than causal because compute is itself an outcome of execution.

A cross-family judge preserves the pooled direction for the three checked contrasts, including \CrossFamilyFullSinglePassWinRate\ for Agentic CogWriter versus \condSinglePass\ and \CrossFamilyFullTaskPlanningWinRate\ versus \condTaskPlanning, but yields a non-tied prompt-collapsed judgment on only \CrossFamilyPooledCommitRate\ of eligible pairs and reverses Agentic CogWriter versus \condSinglePass\ on WritingBench. We therefore treat it as a robustness check rather than replacement confirmatory evidence. Length, compute, and cross-family sensitivity analyses appear in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses}.
