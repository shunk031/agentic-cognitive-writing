\subsection{Direct comparison of writing architectures}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

We next compare outputs written for the same prompt. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports descriptive mean win rates across replications after collapsing the two presentation orders for each prompt. \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to the one-shot, fixed-stage, and adaptive task-planning baselines, with across-benchmark mean win rates of \PooledFourOneRateMean, \PooledFourTwoRateMean, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean, respectively. The direction holds in every benchmark--replication cell against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, and in all but one cell against \condStaged. Replicate-specific sign tests with within-benchmark Holm correction support this pattern; Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise-detail} reports the intervals and cell-level decisions.

\subsection{Which components account for the difference?}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} reports the two single-component interventions and the bundled execution diagnostic. Removing the explicit goal network (\condNoGoals) leaves the pairwise comparison close to even, and no benchmark--replication cell remains significant after Holm correction. Fixing the process order to \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} (\condFixedOrder) yields the same inferential pattern. We therefore do not find evidence that either explicit goal bookkeeping or online process ordering is individually necessary for the observed advantage. These tests also do not establish that the modified systems are equivalent to the full system.

\input{tab/ablation-results}

The \condSingleWriter\ diagnostic produces a much larger difference in favor of \condAgenticCogWriter. That result is not attributable to role separation alone because \condSingleWriter\ simultaneously removes subagent delegation, changes how much text is produced between decisions, and changes output length. The narrower post-hoc \condSingleContext\ run preserves the process loop and shows no significant pooled difference from \condAgenticCogWriter\ in its single exploratory replication (\SingleContextFullRate\ win rate for \condSingleContext, $p=\SingleContextFullP$, unadjusted). Because that run also differs in gateway entry point and transport, it reduces but does not remove the execution confounds. Appendix~\ref{app:single-context} reports the benchmark-level results.

\subsection{Robustness checks}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The main pairwise direction generally survives the tested output-length and compute restrictions where eligible comparisons are available. The primary judge favors longer outputs overall, but \condAgenticCogWriter\ remains favored over \condSinglePass\ within the pre-specified length-matching bands. Under the tightest available compute matching, the direction also remains favorable against \condStaged\ and \condTaskPlanning; no \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ pairs fall in that compute band. These analyses are descriptive because output length and realized compute are consequences of execution.

A cross-family judge preserves the pooled direction for the checked contrasts but produces many ties and reverses the WritingBench direction for \condAgenticCogWriter\ versus \condSinglePass. We therefore treat it as a partial robustness check rather than a replacement for the primary evaluation. Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses} gives the full length, compute, and cross-family results.
