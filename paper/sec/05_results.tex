\label{sec:results-pointwise}

We begin with the benchmark-native scores and standardized pointwise composite, which evaluate each output in isolation. On DoLoMiTes, however, \condStaged\ has the strongest pointwise score. The pointwise results therefore show a competitive process-organized system, not one that dominates every benchmark and metric.

\subsection{Matched-prompt pairwise comparison}
\label{sec:results-effectiveness}

Direct comparison of outputs for the same prompt gives a clearer separation between control organizations. Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} reports descriptive mean win rates across replications after collapsing the two presentation orders for each prompt. \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning, with across-benchmark mean win rates of \PooledFourOneRateMean, \PooledFourTwoRateMean, and \PooledFourThreeRateMean, respectively. The direction holds in every benchmark--replication cell against \condSinglePass\ and \condTaskPlanning, and in all but one cell against \condStaged. Replicate-specific sign tests with within-benchmark Holm correction support this pattern; Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise-detail} reports the intervals and cell-level decisions.

\input{tab/pairwise-results}

These comparisons show that the proposed process-organized system is preferred to the three tested alternatives under the primary pairwise evaluation. Because several architectural features change together across these systems, this comparison alone does not identify which feature produces the difference.

\subsection{Ablations and execution diagnostics}
\label{sec:results-mechanism}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results} tests two components of the full system. Removing the explicit goal network (\condNoGoals) leaves pairwise preference close to even, and no benchmark--replication cell is significant after Holm correction. Replacing runtime process choice with the fixed \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle (\condFixedOrder) gives the same inferential pattern. In other words, under these tests, neither intervention makes the modified system significantly worse than \condAgenticCogWriter. This is a failure to detect a degradation, not evidence that the systems are equivalent.

\input{tab/ablation-results}

The bundled \condSingleWriter\ diagnostic is different: \condAgenticCogWriter\ is strongly preferred. That contrast cannot be attributed to role separation alone because \condSingleWriter\ simultaneously removes delegation, changes the amount of text produced between decisions, and changes output length. The narrower post-hoc \condSingleContext\ check keeps the process loop and does not show a significant pooled difference from \condAgenticCogWriter\ in its single exploratory replication (\SingleContextFullRate\ win rate for \condSingleContext, $p=\SingleContextFullP$, unadjusted). Because that run also changes gateway entry point and transport, it narrows but does not eliminate the execution confounds. Benchmark-level results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:single-context}.

\subsection{How the monitor behaves}
\label{sec:results-process-dynamics}

The execution traces show that the \texttt{Monitor} uses less process-order flexibility than the architecture permits. Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics} summarizes three observable behaviors. Most completed runs follow the canonical \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle; returns from \texttt{Reviewing} to \texttt{Planning} are rare; and accepted goal regeneration is also rare.

\subsection{Robustness checks}
\label{sec:results-robustness}

The main pairwise direction generally survives the tested output-length and compute restrictions where eligible comparisons are available. The same-family judge favors longer outputs overall, but \condAgenticCogWriter\ remains favored over \condSinglePass\ within the pre-specified length-matching bands. Under the tightest available compute matching, the direction also remains favorable against \condStaged\ and \condTaskPlanning; no \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ pairs fall in that compute band. These checks are descriptive because output length and realized compute are consequences of execution rather than randomized treatments.

A cross-family judge preserves the pooled direction for the checked contrasts but produces many ties and reverses the WritingBench direction for \condAgenticCogWriter\ versus \condSinglePass. We therefore use it as a robustness check rather than replacement confirmatory evidence. Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity-analyses} gives the full length, compute, and cross-family results.
