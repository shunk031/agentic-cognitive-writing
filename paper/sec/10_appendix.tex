\section{Additional Process and Resource Statistics}
\label{app:process-behavior}
Table~\ref{tab:process-summary} reports output size, delegated invocations, goal events, token accounting, and wall-clock time, each averaged across the three generation runs.
For \condAgenticCogWriter, a generation run averages \AfourGoalCreatedMean\ goal-creation events and \AfourGoalDevelopedMean\ goal-development events.
The post-\texttt{Reviewing} ledger is the \texttt{Monitor}'s first process-switch decision after each \texttt{Reviewing} pass, which lists every regeneration proposal from the \texttt{Reviewer} with its acceptance or rejection, or states that none was made.
A generation run averages \AfourLedgerEntriesMean\ ledger entries, \AfourLedgerWithProposalMean\ of which contain a regeneration proposal, and the trace records \AfourGoalRegeneratedMean\ accepted goal regenerations (Table~\ref{tab:process-summary}).
\input{tab/appendix-process}

\paragraph{Trace processing.}
Process paths are read from the \texttt{Monitor}'s \texttt{process\_switch} events in trace order: each event's destination process is one step, and a run ends once, after its last step (E in Figure~\ref{fig:process-sequences}).
Figure~\ref{fig:process-sequences}(a) counts these paths as logged, and Figure~\ref{fig:process-sequences}(b,c) counts step-to-step transitions.
Of the \AfourRunCount\ completed runs of our system, \AfourEndBeforeReviewingRuns\ end their logged path at \texttt{Planning} (\AfourEndPlanningRuns) or \texttt{Translating} (\AfourEndTranslatingRuns); in \AfourEndLastReviewerRuns\ of these the last delegated subagent is the \texttt{Reviewer}, so a final review ran without a logged switch, and in the rest the final document is the draft as last written.
For Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics}, immediate repeats of a process are merged (\emph{repeat collapsing}), and when the first delegated role is \texttt{Planning} but the first logged switch names a later process, a leading \texttt{Planning} step is restored (\emph{leading-\texttt{Planning} reconstruction}; \AfourLeadingReconstruction\ such runs for \condAgenticCogWriter).
A path is \emph{cycle-compliant} when the result consists of one or more complete \texttt{Planning}--\texttt{Translating}--\texttt{Reviewing} passes, and returns after \texttt{Reviewing} are counted from this observed order.
\condFixedOrder\ enforces its order through its prompt only, and \AsixCycleCompliantRate\ of its paths are cycle-compliant; the rest skip or repeat a process, for example \texttt{Translating}--\texttt{Reviewing} without \texttt{Planning}.

\paragraph{Delegation fallback.}
The prompts of our system, \condNoGoals, and \condFixedOrder\ let the \texttt{Monitor} perform a role itself when native delegation is unavailable and require it to record that fallback in the trace; the \condTaskPlanning\ prompt has no such rule.
Counting completed runs whose trace decisions mention the fallback, it occurs in \AfourFallbackRuns\ of \AfourRunCount\ runs of our system, \AfiveFallbackRuns\ of \AfiveFallbackCompleted\ of \condNoGoals, and \AsixFallbackRuns\ of \AsixFallbackCompleted\ of \condFixedOrder.
Dropping every pair whose run of our system used the fallback leaves win rates, averaged over generation runs as in Table~\ref{tab:pairwise-results}, of \NoFallbackFullSinglePassRate, \NoFallbackFullStagedRate, and \NoFallbackFullTaskPlanningRate\ against \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning, over \NoFallbackFullSinglePassPairs, \NoFallbackFullStagedPairs, and \NoFallbackFullTaskPlanningPairs\ pairs.
Dropping pairs in which either run used it leaves \NoFallbackFullNoGoalsRate\ against \condNoGoals\ and \NoFallbackFullFixedOrderRate\ against \condFixedOrder, over \NoFallbackFullNoGoalsPairs\ and \NoFallbackFullFixedOrderPairs\ pairs.
The advantage over the alternatives therefore does not rest on runs in which the \texttt{Monitor} performed a role itself.

\subsection{Output-Length Sensitivity}
\label{app:length-control}
Table~\ref{tab:appendix-length-control} reports matched-length win rates for our system separately in each of the three generation runs.
Within the tighter pre-specified matching band, our system is favored over \condSinglePass\ in every run, stays above parity in every run against \condStaged\ and \condFixedOrder, remains above parity against \condTaskPlanning\ while narrowing toward parity, and changes direction across runs against \condNoGoals.
The wider pre-specified band shows the same qualitative pattern.
These matching-band results are descriptive sensitivity checks; ties are excluded from the win-rate denominator, and generation runs are reported separately because they reuse the same prompts.
Following the length check of DoLoMiTes~\citep{malaviya2025dolomites}, a rule that always prefers the longer output agrees with the judge on \LengthRuleWritingRate\ of \LengthRuleWritingPairs\ decided pairs on WritingBench, \LengthRuleHelloRate\ of \LengthRuleHelloPairs\ on HelloBench, and \LengthRuleDoLoRate\ of \LengthRuleDoLoPairs\ on DoLoMiTes, over the pre-specified contrasts and three generation runs.
The \condSingleWriter\ length-band tests in Section~\ref{sec:results-mechanism}, in which \condSingleWriter\ wins \SingleWriterLengthFiveRate\ and \SingleWriterLengthTenRate, instead combine the three runs, so their sign tests take the prompt as the unit: a prompt counts as a win or a loss when its outcomes in the band, averaged across runs, favor one system.

\subsection{Revision-Request Experiment}
\label{app:revision-requests}
This exploratory experiment, specified before generation, tests whether adaptive process selection matters when a finished document must be revised.
It draws \RevisionPrompts\ prompts evenly from the three benchmark subsets; each system first completes the assignment and then, in the same session, receives one change request that adds a constraint, supplies new evidence, or conflicts with part of the assignment.
The change requests were written once by \modelJudgeBare\ and frozen before any system ran; the generator is \modelGen, and the pairwise judge, \modelJudge\ with a revision-specific template, compares the revised documents in both presentation orders and counts an order-inconsistent outcome as a tie.
Each contrast covers the \RevisionFullFixedOrderN\ prompts on which our system completed both turns.
Table~\ref{tab:appendix-revision} shows that our system is not separable from \condFixedOrder\ and is favored over \condStaged\ without a significant difference; the \condSinglePass\ contrast was added outside the plan.
The \texttt{Monitor} responds to the request: its second turn switches to \texttt{Planning} in \RevisionAfourPlanning\ of \RevisionAfourCompleted\ runs and regenerates goals in \RevisionAfourRegenerated, whereas \condFixedOrder\ regenerates goals in \RevisionAsixRegenerated\ of \RevisionAsixCompleted.

The experiment cannot separate adaptive selection from a fixed cycle, because every request arrives after a complete document and calls for replanning, which a fixed order also performs.
The change requests are written by a model and were not validated by people, and the generator, change writer, and judge all belong to the GPT family.
Each system ran once per prompt, and the $p$-values carry no multiplicity correction.

\section{Consensus-Writing Evaluation on Habermas Machine Data}
\label{app:habermas}
We additionally evaluate the writing systems on ten short consensus-writing tasks constructed from the Habermas Machine data~\citep{tessler2024common}.
Each task provides one deliberation question and the five participants' original opinions, and asks the system to write a statement the group could endorse.
The ten tasks are drawn from the \texttt{OOD\_TEST} split after requiring complete, distinct participant opinions and disagreement ratings on both sides of the scale midpoint.
Across eight conditions, this produces 80 attempted runs; 78 complete successfully.
Table~\ref{tab:appendix-habermas} summarizes pointwise quality and the available process statistics.
\input{tab/appendix-habermas}

\condExploratoryOne~\citep{wan2025cognitive} is the exploratory adaptation of CogWriter, with initial planning, immediate plan revision, parallel segment generation, and length review without a goal network; \condExploratoryTwo~\citep{shao2024assisting} is the exploratory adaptation of STORM, with perspective discovery, simulated question answering, outlining, per-section drafting, and polishing without retrieval.
On these short consensus-writing tasks, \condAgenticCogWriter\ does not achieve the highest pointwise composite; in particular, it scores below \condSinglePass\ and \condStaged.
Neither our system nor \condFixedOrder\ regenerates a goal on these tasks, and their post-\texttt{Reviewing} ledgers record \HabAfourLedger\ and \HabAsixLedger\ no/proposal outcomes.
