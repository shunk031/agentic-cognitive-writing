\paragraph{Long-form writing with language models.}
Long-form generation has moved from one-shot prompting toward systems that explicitly organize planning, drafting, and revision. Plan-and-Write~\cite{yao2019plan} separates planning from story generation. Re$^3$~\cite{yang2022re3} constructs an overarching plan, drafts passages against that plan, and then reranks and edits continuations. STORM~\cite{shao2024assisting} emphasizes the pre-writing stage, using multi-perspective research to build an outline before section-by-section article generation. LongWriter's AgentWrite~\cite{bai2025longwriter} decomposes a long document into shorter writing subtasks. More recently, WriteHERE~\cite{xiong2025beyond} makes the workflow adaptive by recursively expanding and scheduling a heterogeneous task graph containing retrieval, reasoning, and composition nodes. IS-CoT~\cite{sun2026cot} instead embeds a recurring Plan--Write--Reflect cycle inside generation. These systems differ not only in how much structure they introduce, but in \emph{what unit advances generation}: a planned passage, an outline section, a task node, or a prescribed process stage.

\paragraph{Iteration, feedback, and cognitively inspired writing.}
Iterative refinement methods also improve generation by alternating production and feedback. Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023self}, for example, repeatedly generates feedback and refines a previous output. Cognitive models of writing provide a richer account of why such iteration may matter. Flower and Hayes~\cite{flower1981cognitive} describe writing as recurrent coordination among planning, translating ideas into text, and reviewing, under a monitoring process; later models revise and extend this account~\cite{hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling}. CogWriter~\cite{wan2025cognitive} is the closest autonomous long-form system to this motivation: it combines hierarchical planning, parallel generation agents, monitoring, and review, including dynamic plan adjustment and content revision.

\paragraph{Our position.}
Our contribution is not that planning, revision, multi-agent execution, or cognitive inspiration are individually new. We focus on a different systems question: \emph{should the writing processes themselves be the actions that an agent can choose next?} Existing adaptive systems such as WriteHERE~\cite{xiong2025beyond} choose among task nodes, while process-oriented systems such as IS-CoT~\cite{sun2026cot} execute a recurring process cycle. \condAgenticCogWriter\ instead makes planning, drafting, and reviewing first-class runtime choices coordinated by a monitor.
