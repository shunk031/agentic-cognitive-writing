\paragraph{Agents that produce long-form artifacts.}
Language agents are increasingly used for multi-step work in which writing is one stage of a larger process. Agent Laboratory~\cite{schmidgall2025agentlab} moves from literature review and experimentation to report writing, while the AI Scientist~\cite{lu2024aiscientist} generates and evaluates full scientific papers after running experiments. These systems motivate our setting: a general-purpose agent can already plan, use tools, maintain files, and delegate work, but those capabilities do not by themselves determine how the act of writing should be organized. Our focus is the architecture of that writing loop inside an agentic harness.

\paragraph{Structured long-form writing.}
Long-form generation systems differ in what they advance from one step to the next. Plan-and-Write~\cite{yao2019plan} separates planning from story generation. Re$^3$~\cite{yang2022re3} constructs a plan, drafts passages against it, and reranks or edits continuations. STORM~\cite{shao2024assisting} emphasizes pre-writing research and outline construction before section-by-section article generation. LongWriter's AgentWrite~\cite{bai2025longwriter} decomposes a long document into shorter writing subtasks. WriteHERE~\cite{xiong2025beyond} makes execution adaptive by recursively expanding and scheduling a heterogeneous task graph of retrieval, reasoning, and composition nodes. IS-CoT~\cite{sun2026cot} instead interleaves Plan--Write--Reflect in a recurring cycle. These approaches show that long-form generation benefits from structure, but the structure is usually attached to a plan element, section, task node, or prescribed stage sequence.

\paragraph{Iteration and cognitive models of writing.}
Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023self} shows that iterative feedback and revision can improve generation without committing to a domain-specific long-form architecture. Writing research provides a more specific account of the activities being coordinated. Flower and Hayes~\cite{flower1981cognitive} model writing as recurrent interaction among planning, translating ideas into text, and reviewing under a monitoring process; later work revises and extends this account~\cite{hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling}. CogWriter~\cite{wan2025cognitive} is the closest autonomous long-form system to this motivation, combining hierarchical planning, parallel generation agents, monitoring, and review under cognitive-writing inspiration.

\paragraph{Position of this work.}
Our contribution is not that agents can plan, revise, delegate, or draw inspiration from cognitive theory. We study a narrower architectural choice: whether \emph{writing processes themselves} should be the actions that determine what happens next. Adaptive task systems such as WriteHERE~\cite{xiong2025beyond} choose among task nodes; process-oriented systems such as IS-CoT~\cite{sun2026cot} prescribe a recurring process cycle. \condAgenticCogWriter\ instead lets a host agent select planning, drafting, or reviewing online from the current writing state.
