\subsection{Cognitive process models of writing}

Cognitive writing models treat composition as more than linear text production. \citet{flower1981cognitive} describe interacting \texttt{Planning}, \texttt{Translating}, and \texttt{Reviewing} processes coordinated by a \texttt{Monitor} and guided by writer-generated goals. Later accounts distinguish knowledge-telling from knowledge-transforming~\cite{bereiter1987psychology}, treat text production as knowledge generation~\cite{galbraith1999writing}, and revise the 1981 model~\cite{hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling}. We use the 1981 account for its explicit process-control hypotheses, not as a complete theory of cognition.

\subsection{Task-oriented control for language-model writing}

Long-form generation research organizes what a language model does. Plan-and-Write~\cite{yao2019plan} separates planning from realization; Re$^3$~\cite{yang2022re3} drafts from a global plan; Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023self} iterates feedback and revision; STORM~\cite{shao2024assisting} strengthens pre-writing; and LongWriter's AgentWrite pipeline~\cite{bai2025longwriter} decomposes long generation into subtasks. These systems control executable work units such as plans, passages, sections, or refinements.

Recent systems make those structures adaptive. WriteHERE~\cite{xiong2025beyond} interleaves recursive task decomposition with execution, and IS-CoT~\cite{sun2026cot} runs a Plan, Write, Reflect cycle in which reflection checks progress and adjusts the next plan. Both decide over units of work; Agentic CogWriter decides which writing process runs next, and our fixed schedule is not a stand-in for IS-CoT.

\subsection{Process-oriented control for writing agents}

Cognitive writing processes have mainly guided writing-support research. \citet{gero2022design} organize support systems around Planning, Translating, and Reviewing; CoCo Matrix~\cite{wan2024coco} characterizes human--AI co-writing; Script\&Shift~\cite{siddiqui2025script} supports movement between content development and rhetorical organization; and other systems infer process states or expose writing histories~\cite{zhou2026iris}. These systems do not make process selection the autonomous controller.

CogWriter~\cite{wan2025cognitive} is closest to ours in autonomous generation. It combines hierarchical planning, generation agents, monitoring, and reviewing, but centers computation on task decomposition and local plans. Of the four systems, none exposes an explicit writer-goal state or a process-level trace (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism-comparison}). Our \texttt{Monitor} instead chooses among \texttt{Planning}, \texttt{Translating}, and \texttt{Reviewing} while maintaining a writer-goal representation. This process-level control also relates to cognitive architectures for language agents~\cite{sumers2024cognitive} and interleaved agent loops~\cite{yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion}.
