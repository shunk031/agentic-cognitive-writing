\paragraph{Language agents and document production.}
Language-agent research studies systems that interleave reasoning with actions, tools, memory, and feedback~\citep{yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion,sumers2024cognitive}, while writing-support systems assist planning, drafting, and revision~\citep{gero2022design}. Autonomous research systems increasingly connect those capabilities to report or paper generation after literature review and experimentation~\citep{schmidgall2025agentlab,lu2024aiscientist}. We focus on how a general-purpose agent organizes that writing stage. Better control of document production can improve not only standalone writing systems but also larger autonomous workflows whose final value depends on the quality of the report or manuscript they produce.

\paragraph{Long-form generation and planning.}
Long-form generation becomes harder to control and evaluate as outputs grow in length~\citep{tan2024proxyqa,wu2026superwriter}, motivating explicit planning and decomposition~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3,shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter,wan2025cognitive,xiong2025beyond,sun2026cot}. Plan-and-Write~\citep{yao2019plan}, Re$^3$~\citep{yang2022re3}, STORM~\citep{shao2024assisting}, and LongWriter~\citep{bai2025longwriter} organize generation around plans, revisions, sections, or manageable content units. CogWriter~\citep{wan2025cognitive}, WriteHERE~\citep{xiong2025beyond}, IS-CoT~\citep{sun2026cot}, and SuperWriter~\citep{wu2026superwriter} make structure or refinement increasingly adaptive. CogWriter conditionally revises plans and generated segments within a planning-and-generation workflow, whereas WriteHERE schedules nodes in an evolving heterogeneous task graph. Our \condAgenticCogWriter\ instead makes the next writing process, whether planning, drafting, or reviewing, the document-level control decision.

\paragraph{Iteration and cognitive models of writing.}
Iterative generation methods improve text through repeated feedback and revision~\citep{madaan2023self,du2022iterative}, while cognitive writing research offers a process-level account of how planning, drafting, and reviewing interact~\citep{flower1981cognitive,hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling}. \citet{gero2022design} use those processes to organize writing-support systems, and CogWriter~\citep{wan2025cognitive} likewise draws on cognitive writing theory to structure planning, generation, and revision. Our \condAgenticCogWriter\ differs by making the writing process itself the online action chosen by a shared document-level controller and by testing that control choice with matched alternatives, ablations, and analyses of observed writing behavior.
