\paragraph{Language agents and document production.}
Language-agent research has focused on architectures that interleave reasoning with actions, tools, memory, and feedback~\citep{yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion,sumers2024cognitive}. Writing is increasingly one output of such systems rather than a standalone language-model call. Writing-support research studies how computational tools can assist planning, drafting, and revision~\citep{gero2022design}, while agentic research systems integrate literature review, experimentation, and report or paper generation in a larger workflow~\citep{schmidgall2025agentlab,lu2024aiscientist}. Our setting lies between these lines of work: we study the internal organization of the writing stage when a general-purpose agent is responsible for producing the document.

\paragraph{Long-form generation and planning.}
Long-form generation systems use explicit structure to avoid the coherence and constraint failures of single-pass generation~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3,shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter,wan2025cognitive,xiong2025beyond,sun2026cot}. Plan-and-Write~\citep{yao2019plan} separates planning from realization; Re$^3$~\citep{yang2022re3} recursively prompts and revises long stories; STORM~\citep{shao2024assisting} uses research and outlining before section-wise article generation; and LongWriter~\citep{bai2025longwriter} decomposes long outputs into manageable generation units. More recent systems make structure itself adaptive: CogWriter~\citep{wan2025cognitive} couples hierarchical planning with validation and revision, WriteHERE~\citep{xiong2025beyond} evolves a heterogeneous task graph, and IS-CoT~\citep{sun2026cot} interleaves structural thinking with generation. The central distinction in our work is the unit selected during execution: these systems primarily advance content, plan, or task structure, whereas \condAgenticCogWriter\ selects among named writing processes.

\paragraph{Iteration and cognitive models of writing.}
Writing and revision are inherently iterative, and computational work has studied both repeated model feedback and human revision behavior~\citep{madaan2023self,du2022iterative}. Self-Refine~\citep{madaan2023self} alternates generation, feedback, and refinement without requiring a task-specific process model, while \citet{du2022iterative} model revision as a sequence of edit intentions across domains and granularities. Cognitive writing research provides a complementary decomposition: \citet{flower1981cognitive} distinguish \texttt{Planning}, \texttt{Translating}, and \texttt{Reviewing}, and \citet{gero2022design} use these processes to organize a systematic review of writing-support systems. CogWriter~\citep{wan2025cognitive} also draws on cognitive writing theory, but uses it to structure planning, generation, and revision modules. We instead make the writing process itself the online action selected by the agent and then test that architectural choice through matched alternatives, ablations, and traces.
