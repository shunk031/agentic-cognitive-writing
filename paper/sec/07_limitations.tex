\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope and external validity.}
Our conclusions concern one generator family, one coding-agent-style harness, and deterministic demanding subsets of three long-form benchmarks.
The subset rule emphasizes long inputs and, when specified, long requested outputs rather than an independent semantic measure of difficulty.
The results therefore do not establish transfer to shorter tasks, objectives that change during writing, other model families or runtimes, interactive human--AI writing, or end-to-end agents with retrieval.
The alternative writing systems are matched implementations under our common generator and harness, not executions of the published systems.

\paragraph{Evaluator dependence.}
The primary evaluation also leaves uncertainty about judge dependence.
Pairwise win rates are computed over order-consistent comparisons, where both presentation orders select the same response; Section~\ref{sec:results-effectiveness} also reports unconditional and worst-case missing-output views.
The primary evaluator belongs to the same model family as the generator, and related generator--judge models can exhibit preference leakage or self-preference~\citep{li2026preference,panickssery2024selfpreference}.
The cross-family check produces many ties and reverses one WritingBench direction, and we did not collect human judgments, so model-judge preference should not be treated as human preference.
The primary evaluator also favors longer outputs overall.

\paragraph{Component attribution.}
The component analyses constrain mechanism claims without establishing equivalence.
Removing the explicit goal representation or fixing process order produces no reliable difference in the pre-specified multiple-comparison-corrected tests, while the exploratory prompt-level summary estimates a small preference for adaptive ordering and no comparable goal-representation effect.
\condSingleWriter\ changes delegation, role context, decision granularity, and output length together; \condSingleContext\ is narrower but was added post-hoc, uses one exploratory generation run, and differs in execution interface and transport.
The current evidence therefore cannot attribute the remaining system-level advantage to any single shared execution feature.

\paragraph{Descriptive execution analyses.}
The length- and compute-matched analyses are descriptive because both variables are consequences of execution.
Process traces likewise record implemented state transitions and agent actions; they characterize how \condAgenticCogWriter\ behaves on these tasks but do not measure human cognition or validate cognitive process theory as a model of language-agent internals.
