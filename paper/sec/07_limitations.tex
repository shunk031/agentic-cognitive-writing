\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our conclusions concern one generator family, one coding-agent-style harness, and long-output slices from three writing benchmarks. They do not establish that the same architecture will transfer to other model families, agent runtimes, shorter writing tasks, interactive human--AI writing, or end-to-end research agents operating with retrieval and external tools. The selected prompts emphasize requested output length rather than an independent measure of task difficulty, so the benchmark results should be read as evidence about demanding long-form settings rather than the full benchmark distributions.

The alternative architectures are controlled re-implementations under our common generator and harness, not executions of the published systems. Their purpose is to isolate broad organizational patterns such as one-shot generation, fixed stages, and adaptive task planning. The reported numbers therefore should not be interpreted as evaluations of the original systems or compared directly with their published leaderboard results.

The pointwise and benchmark-native scores are descriptive product diagnostics from the initial replication rather than replicated confirmatory outcomes. The primary comparative evidence comes from matched-output judging. Completion differs across replications, and pairwise analyses exclude prompts for which either system lacks a completed output. Although that exclusion is symmetric, the resulting preferences describe completed output pairs rather than all attempted runs.

The primary pairwise judge shares the generator's model family even though the locked analysis plan specified a different-family confirmatory judge. The cross-family judge provides only a partial robustness check: it returns many ties and reverses the WritingBench direction for the \condAgenticCogWriter--\condSinglePass\ comparison. We also do not have human preference judgments. In addition, the primary judge favors longer outputs overall, so pairwise preference should not be interpreted as a pure measure of writing quality or as evidence for a particular mechanism.

The component interventions identify only what the current experiments fail to require. A non-significant difference after removing the goal network or fixing process order does not establish equivalence between those systems and \condAgenticCogWriter. The \condSingleWriter\ diagnostic changes role context, delegation, control granularity, and output length together. The post-hoc \condSingleContext\ comparison is narrower but uses a different gateway entry point and plain HTTP transport and was run for only one exploratory replication. The current evidence therefore cannot attribute the remaining performance difference to role separation, delegation, repeated checkpoints, persistent state, or any other single shared execution feature.

The length- and compute-matched analyses are descriptive robustness checks rather than causal adjustments. Output length and inference compute are consequences of execution, and the tightest compute band does not contain eligible pairs for every primary comparison. Finally, the process traces record implemented system state and control events. They help characterize how this agent behaves, but they are not measurements of human cognition and do not validate Flower and Hayes' theory as a model of language-agent internals.
