\section*{Limitations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope and external validity.}
Our conclusions concern one generator family, one coding-agent-style harness, and deterministic subsets of three long-form benchmarks.
The subset rule emphasizes long inputs and, when specified, long requested outputs rather than an independent measure of difficulty or of the need to revise.
The results therefore do not establish transfer to shorter tasks, other model families or runtimes, interactive human--AI writing, or end-to-end agents with retrieval.
A direct next step is a task set that changes objectives, introduces new evidence, or adds rhetorical constraints during writing, so that process selection is required rather than only available.

\paragraph{Comparison design.}
The alternative writing systems are reimplementations under the shared setup of Section~\ref{sec:implementation}, not executions of the published systems.
\condTaskPlanning\ also delegates its task nodes to subagents, so delegation is present on both sides of that comparison.
CogWriter-style and STORM-style adaptations (\condExploratoryOne, \condExploratoryTwo) run only on ten consensus-writing tasks, and running them on the three benchmarks is the next comparison.\footnote{See Appendix~\ref{app:habermas} for the consensus-writing results.}
The compute-matched analysis selects pairs with similar realized token use after generation; a Self-Refine baseline~\citep{madaan2023self} given our system's token budget before generation would test budget-matched iterative revision directly.

\paragraph{Evaluator dependence.}
Section~\ref{sec:results-effectiveness} complements the order-consistent win rates with unconditional and worst-case missing-output views.
The primary evaluator belongs to the same model family as the generator, and related generator--judge models can exhibit preference leakage or self-preference~\citep{li2026preference,panickssery2024selfpreference}.
The cross-family judge rescored one generation run for the contrasts with \condSinglePass, \condTaskPlanning, and \condNoGoals; summing W/L/T counts over the three benchmarks leaves all three favoring our system, but the judge produces many ties and reverses 3 of 9 benchmark-level comparisons.
Extending the check to \condStaged\ and \condFixedOrder\ and to all three generation runs would cover every contrast between our system and an alternative or ablation.
Model-judge preference is not human preference; a blinded human comparison of same-prompt outputs, stratified by benchmark, contrast, and prompt length, would measure how closely the judges agree with human readers.

\paragraph{Component attribution.}
The ablations constrain explicit goals and adaptive ordering to modest contributions without establishing equivalence.
Neither ablation differs from the full system in any benchmark--run test after Holm correction, each such test reaches 80\% power~\citep{card2020little} at a true preference of about \PowerCellDetectablePreference, and the exploratory prompt-level summary does not rank the two components.\footnote{See Appendix~\ref{app:prompts} for the power calculation.}
\condFixedOrder\ enforces its cycle through the \texttt{Monitor}'s instructions so that it shares our system's agent runtime and differs only in the selection rule, and \AsixCycleCompliantRate\ of its paths are cycle-compliant.\footnote{See Appendix~\ref{app:process-behavior} for the trace classification.}
Next steps are a runner-enforced cycle and a combined ablation that removes goals and fixes the order together, which would test whether the two mechanisms substitute for each other.

\paragraph{Execution variants.}
\condSingleWriter\ changes delegation, role context, decision granularity, and output length together; \condSingleContext\ is narrower but was added post-hoc, uses one exploratory generation run, and differs in execution interface and transport.
The current evidence therefore does not attribute the remaining system-level advantage to any single shared execution feature.

\paragraph{Descriptive execution analyses.}
The primary evaluator favors longer outputs overall, and the length- and compute-matched analyses are descriptive because both variables result from execution.
Process traces likewise record state transitions and agent actions; they characterize how \condAgenticCogWriter\ behaves on these tasks but do not measure human cognition or validate cognitive process theory as a model of language-agent internals.
