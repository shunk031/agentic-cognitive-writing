In Section~\ref{sec:results-mechanism}, \condNoGoals, \condFixedOrder, and \condSingleContext\ stay near parity with \condAgenticCogWriter, whereas \condStagedRoles, which adds the same role instructions to the fixed pipeline, does not reproduce its advantage.
The advantage over the alternatives stays above parity in the logistic length model and in the descriptive compute-matched subsets.
Adaptive ordering adds only the small advantage over \condFixedOrder\ seen in the post-hoc prompt-level summary, and our system seldom uses it on these tasks, where most traces show one planning--drafting--reviewing cycle.
Which shared component, if any, carries the advantage is untested; the combined ablation named in the Limitations, which removes goals, fixes the order, and drops subagents together, is that test.
