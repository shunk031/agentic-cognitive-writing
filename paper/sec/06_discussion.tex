The component results of Section~\ref{sec:results-mechanism} suggest why \condAgenticCogWriter\ wins: \condNoGoals, \condFixedOrder, and \condSingleContext\ keep its \texttt{Monitor}-run loop over a persistent draft and stay near parity, whereas \condStagedRoles\ receives the same role instructions but rewrites the whole response once and does not.
The advantage is therefore more consistent with that loop than with goals, subagent contexts, or instruction content alone, and it stays above parity in the logistic length model and the descriptive compute-matched subsets.
Adaptive ordering adds only a small post-hoc advantage over \condFixedOrder, and the loop seldom uses it on these tasks, where most traces show one cycle.
