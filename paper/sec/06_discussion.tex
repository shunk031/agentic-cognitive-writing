Tables~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} and~\ref{tab:ablation-results} suggest why \condAgenticCogWriter\ wins: \condNoGoals, \condFixedOrder, and \condSingleContext\ keep its \texttt{Monitor}-run loop over a persistent draft and stay near parity, whereas \condStagedRoles\ receives the same role instructions but rewrites the whole response once and does not.
The advantage is therefore more consistent with that loop than with goals, adaptive ordering, subagent contexts, or instruction content alone, and it persists at equal length and within matched compute bands.
Because most traces show one planning--drafting--reviewing cycle, the loop seldom uses its adaptive ordering on these tasks.
