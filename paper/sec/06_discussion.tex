\paragraph{What the results identify.}
The main comparison supports writing-process choice as a useful action space for long-form generation, but the ablations do not support the more elaborate control mechanisms as its explanation. Removing the explicit goal network produces a bounded null, fixing the process order yields no Holm-surviving benchmark cell, and the unrestricted \texttt{Monitor} itself follows the canonical \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle in \AfourCycleCompliantRate\ of completed runs. It returns from \texttt{Reviewing} to \texttt{Planning} in only \AfourReviewingToPlanningRate\ of runs and accepts goal regeneration in only \AfourRegenerationRuns\ of \AfourRunCount\ runs. The current evidence therefore localizes the observed advantage away from explicit goal bookkeeping and adaptive process scheduling. It does \emph{not} yet identify what remains: \condSingleWriter\ changes role separation, recursion granularity, and output length together.

\paragraph{Why might process structure help?}
Several mechanisms remain plausible. Separate process executions may create cleaner role-conditioned contexts, reduce interference between planning, drafting, and reviewing objectives, or introduce useful checkpoints even when their order is nearly fixed. The result is not explained by delegation frequency alone: \condTaskPlanning\ spawns more delegated work than \condFull\ yet loses the pairwise comparison. Nor is it straightforwardly explained by greater inference expenditure: within the 1.25 compute-ratio band, \condFull\ remains preferred to \condStaged\ at \ComputeFullStagedWithinOneTwentyFiveRate\ and to \condTaskPlanning\ at \ComputeFullTaskPlanningWithinOneTwentyFiveRate. These observations narrow the space of explanations but do not distinguish process decomposition from context separation or recursion granularity.

\paragraph{Implications and scope.}
For language-agent design, the result suggests that choosing a useful \emph{action space}, meaning what the controller is allowed to decide next, may matter more than making the controller increasingly dynamic. For the source theory, the implementation exercised the mechanisms that make its control account distinctive, yet the product differences did not depend detectably on explicit goal state or flexible process order. One interpretation is therefore that the theory is computationally useful as a decomposition of writing activity even when its dynamic scheduling commitments add little in the present setting. This is an engineering result, not evidence about human cognition: the recorded goals and process switches are agent state. The empirical scope is also narrow. The primary judge shares the generator's model family; the cross-family judge preserves pooled directions but commits on only \CrossFamilyPooledCommitRate\ of pairs and reverses \condFull\ versus \condSinglePass\ on WritingBench. All generation uses one model family, and the evaluation focuses on the longest-output slice of the three benchmarks. These limitations bound the claim to the tested systems and evaluation regime.
