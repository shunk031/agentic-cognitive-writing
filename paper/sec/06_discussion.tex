Tables~\ref{tab:main-results} and~\ref{tab:pairwise-results} show that \condAgenticCogWriter\ is distinguished by consistent same-prompt preference over the three alternatives rather than uniform pointwise dominance.
Because the ablations keep most of that advantage, neither explicit goals nor adaptive ordering alone is required for it on these tasks, where the traces show little replanning.

Figure~\ref{fig:process-sequences} and Table~\ref{tab:process-dynamics} show that \condAgenticCogWriter\ usually follows planning--drafting--reviewing and departs selectively, so process-level control is not the same as continual replanning.
This complements systems that adapt task decomposition or structural reasoning during generation~\citep{xiong2025beyond,wan2025cognitive,sun2026cot}: a second axis is which process a controller selects, not only how dynamically it replans.
More broadly, cognitive writing theory provides executable control abstractions rather than claims that language agents reproduce human cognition, making alternative writing processes explicit enough to implement, compare, and falsify.
