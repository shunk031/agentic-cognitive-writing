\subsection{What does the performance pattern establish?}

The matched-prompt evaluation provides evidence of a performance difference between the tested organizations under our generator, harness, prompt slice, and evaluation protocol; the secondary product-level diagnostics show that \condAgenticCogWriter\ is competitive rather than uniformly best. Neither result identifies the process-level action space itself as the causal source of the difference.

\subsection{What explains the remaining advantage?}

The confirmatory interventions do not identify explicit goal representation or adaptive process ordering as its source. The unrestricted \texttt{Monitor} usually follows the canonical \texttt{Planning}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Translating}$\rightarrow$\texttt{Reviewing} cycle, and departures from that cycle do not track a larger pairwise advantage. Accepted goal regeneration is rare, but goals are still created and developed, so the traces do not show that the explicit goal representation is inactive.

The diagnostic \condSingleWriter\ result also does not isolate a mechanism: it bundles role-context separation, delegation, control granularity, and output length. The narrower \condSingleContext\ check retains the process loop and shows no detectable difference after pooling benchmark outcomes within its exploratory replication, but it also differs in gateway entry point and transport. It therefore constrains a simple role-context explanation without providing a clean context-only intervention. Delegation count does not track preference because \condTaskPlanning\ delegates more work yet loses the pairwise comparison. Compute- and length-matched checks likewise preserve key directions. These analyses constrain simple explanations but are not causal mediation estimates.

\subsection{Implications}

The remaining candidates are features shared across the successful process-organized conditions, including explicit process decomposition, repeated returns through the \texttt{Monitor}, and multi-step execution. Direct intervention on those features is the next step. More broadly, the cognitive process theory is useful here less as a blueprint than as a source of executable commitments: its named processes supply a computational decomposition, while mutable goals and flexible ordering become choices that can be tested separately. The null results narrow which theory-derived commitments plausibly account for the observed gain under this design; they are an engineering result about language-agent organization, not evidence about the necessity of corresponding mechanisms in human writing.
