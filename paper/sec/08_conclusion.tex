\condAgenticCogWriter\ reframes long-form writing for AI agents as process-level control: a top-level \texttt{Monitor} selects planning, drafting, or reviewing from persistent state and delegates to role-specific subagents.
Across three long-form writing benchmarks, our system is preferred to single-pass generation, fixed writing stages, and adaptive task planning in same-prompt comparisons; neither explicit goals nor adaptive process ordering alone explains the advantage, and most traces follow one planning--drafting--reviewing cycle.
These results support process-level control for long-form writing agents and show how cognitive writing theory can yield executable, falsifiable system hypotheses.
