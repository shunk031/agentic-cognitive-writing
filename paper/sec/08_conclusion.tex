We use the cognitive process theory of writing to pose testable hypotheses about language-agent control. \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning\ on the tested long-form slices, yet the confirmatory interventions do not identify explicit goal representation or adaptive process ordering as sources of that advantage, and lower-tier role-context checks do not isolate an alternative mechanism. The remaining candidates are features shared by the successful process-organized conditions, such as process decomposition and repeated returns through the \texttt{Monitor}. More broadly, the results show the value of making theory-derived commitments executable and testing them separately rather than adopting a cognitive architecture wholesale.
