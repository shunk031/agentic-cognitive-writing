We use the cognitive process theory of writing to pose testable hypotheses about language-agent control rather than to import a cognitive architecture wholesale. \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to single-pass generation, fixed writing stages, and adaptive task planning on the tested long-form benchmark subsets, yet the component analyses do not identify explicit goal representation or adaptive process ordering as sources of that advantage.

The broader lesson is methodological: cognitive theory becomes useful for language-agent design when its commitments can be implemented and tested separately. Our results support process-organized, stateful writing as a promising system design while leaving the mechanism behind its advantage open.
