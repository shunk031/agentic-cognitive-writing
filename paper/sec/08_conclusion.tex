We proposed \condAgenticCogWriter, a coding-agent-based system that organizes long-form writing around repeated choices among planning, drafting, and reviewing. Across three long-form benchmarks, the system is competitive under pointwise scoring and is preferred in matched-prompt comparisons to one-shot generation, a fixed staged workflow, and adaptive task planning under our primary evaluation.

The component analyses complicate a simple mechanism story. Removing the explicit goal representation or forcing a fixed planning--drafting--reviewing cycle does not significantly worsen pairwise preference, and the unrestricted \texttt{Monitor} often follows that same cycle. The results therefore support the process-organized system without showing that explicit goals or adaptive process ordering alone explain its advantage. More generally, they show how a theory of writing can be turned into concrete agent-design choices that can be tested rather than assumed.
