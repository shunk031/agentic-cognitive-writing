Recent coding agents have made tool-using, stateful execution prominent, and similar workflows increasingly produce long-form documents.
Yet long-form writing systems usually advance an outline, task graph, or fixed stage sequence rather than decide which writing process is needed next.
Drawing on cognitive process theory, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, in which a Monitor agent chooses and delegates planning, drafting, or reviewing from a shared draft, goals, and process history.
We evaluate our system across three long-form writing benchmarks using pointwise rubric scoring and pairwise same-prompt preference judgments.
Our system is competitive in pointwise quality and preferred to one-shot generation, fixed planning--drafting--revision, and adaptive task planning in pairwise evaluation.
Ablations support a system-level interpretation rather than attributing the advantage to explicit goals or adaptive process order alone.
Process traces show that one planning--drafting--reviewing cycle is typical, with departures used selectively.
The results support process-level control for long-form writing agents.
