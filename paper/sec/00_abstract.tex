Recent coding agents increasingly produce long-form documents, yet how such an agent should organize its writing is an open question.
Long-form writing systems fix each step's unit in advance (an outline section, task node, or stage), so the writer does not choose the next writing process.
Motivated by cognitive process theory, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, an agent skill whose Monitor agent keeps a shared draft, goals, and process history and delegates planning, drafting, or reviewing to subagents.
We evaluate our system on three long-form writing benchmarks with pointwise rubric scores and pairwise preferences under a matched generator, harness, and budget.
Pre-specified pairwise tests prefer it to one-shot generation in most tests, but to a fixed planning--drafting--revision workflow and adaptive task planning in fewer than half; a post-hoc prompt-level summary favors it over all three, as do paired pointwise differences on WritingBench and HelloBench's native rubric.
Adding our role instructions to the fixed workflow post hoc does not reproduce this advantage.
Removing explicit goals or fixing the process order keeps most of it, and process traces show that \AfourCycleCompliantRate\ of complete paths conform to the planning--drafting--reviewing cycle structure and \AfourReviewingToPlanningRate\ of runs return to planning, so adaptive control is seldom exercised.
