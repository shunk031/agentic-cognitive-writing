Recent coding agents write long-form documents, yet in our runs a plain agent allowed to revise its draft usually wrote it once.
Long-form systems instead fix each step's unit in advance, so the writer does not choose the next writing process.
Motivated by cognitive process theory, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, a writing skill whose Monitor agent keeps a shared draft and goals and delegates planning, drafting, or reviewing.
We evaluate our system on three long-form writing benchmarks with pointwise rubric scores and pairwise preferences.
Pre-specified tests prefer it to one-shot generation in most tests but to a fixed workflow and task planning in fewer than half; post-hoc analyses, and paired pointwise differences on WritingBench and on HelloBench's native rubric, favor it over all three, and a post-hoc test favors it over the plain agent overall.
Adding our role instructions to the fixed workflow post hoc does not reproduce this advantage.
A post-hoc ablation removing goals, adaptive ordering, and subagents stays at parity and beats the fixed workflow, tying the advantage on these tasks to the Monitor's role-guided pass over a persistent draft.
Process traces show \AfourCycleCompliantRate\ of complete paths follow the planning--drafting--reviewing cycle, so benchmarks requiring adaptive selection are next.
