Large language model agents~\citep{yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion,sumers2024cognitive,luo2025llmagent} increasingly combine reasoning, tools, memory, and delegation for multi-step knowledge work.
Recent coding agents have made this tool-mediated execution style especially prominent~\citep{yang2024sweagent,wang2024openhands}, while similar workflows increasingly produce reports and scientific manuscripts~\citep{schmidgall2025agentlab,lu2024aiscientist}.
For document-producing agents, long-form writing therefore requires coordinating organization, drafting, inspection, and revision rather than merely generating final text.

Existing long-form writing systems structure generation through plans, document units, and task decomposition~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3,shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter,xiong2025beyond}.
Some build outlines or generate section by section~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3,shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter}, some recursively decompose a document into tasks~\citep{xiong2025beyond}, and some adapt plans or structural reasoning during generation~\citep{wan2025cognitive,sun2026cot}.
Across these approaches, the next step is usually a content unit, task node, or prescribed stage.
A complementary question remains: should the writer instead decide which kind of writing process is needed next?

The cognitive process theory of writing~\citep{flower1981cognitive,hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling} offers a process-level control abstraction.
\citet{flower1981cognitive} characterize writing as coordination among planning, translating, and reviewing, with a monitor deciding which process to invoke as the document develops.
The theory therefore suggests choosing the next writing process rather than the next section or task node.

Building on \citet{flower1981cognitive}, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, a long-form writing system that applies this control structure in a coding-agent-style harness with persistent state and subagent delegation (Figure~\ref{fig:agentic-cogwriter}).
A top-level Monitor agent observes the draft, goals, and process history, chooses planning, drafting, or reviewing, and delegates the selected activity to the corresponding subagent.
The resulting process order is selected during writing rather than fixed in advance.

We evaluate our system on demanding subsets of three complementary long-form writing benchmarks spanning broad instruction-driven writing, long-context generation, and methodical expert-document production~\citep{wu2026writingbench,que2024hellobench,malaviya2025dolomites}.
We hold the generator and task information constant across the compared conditions and assess quality with pointwise rubric scoring and pairwise same-prompt preference evaluation.
Our system is preferred to one-shot generation, a fixed writing pipeline, and adaptive task planning; pointwise scores are competitive.
The ablations support a system-level interpretation rather than attributing the advantage to explicit goals or adaptive ordering alone, while process traces show that adaptive ordering is used selectively around a common planning--drafting--reviewing path.

Our contributions are threefold.
We formulate long-form writing for general-purpose agents as a process-level control problem and introduce a system that chooses among planning, drafting, and reviewing; we compare our system with one-shot, fixed-stage, and adaptive task-planning alternatives across complementary long-form benchmarks; and we use ablations and process analyses to test proposed components and characterize the writing loop.
