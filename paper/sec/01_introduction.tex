Large language model agents~\citep{yao2023react,shinn2023reflexion,sumers2024cognitive,luo2025llmagent} increasingly combine reasoning, tool use, memory, and delegation to carry out multi-step knowledge work.
Recent coding agents have made this interactive, tool-mediated style of execution especially prominent~\citep{yang2024sweagent,wang2024openhands}, while similar agentic workflows increasingly produce reports or scientific manuscripts~\citep{schmidgall2025agentlab,lu2024aiscientist}.
For agents that produce documents, long-form writing requires more than final text generation: the agent must organize the document, turn partial plans into prose, inspect what has been written, and decide when revision is needed.
On its own, a coding agent may not organize them: in our harness, a plain agent allowed to plan, draft, and revise a draft file wrote it once in \PlainAgentWrittenOnceRuns\ of \PlainAgentCompletedRuns\ runs, and most others only adjusted length.

Existing long-form writing systems impose different forms of structure on generation~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3,shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter,xiong2025beyond,sun2026cot}.
Some build an outline before drafting~\citep{yao2019plan,yang2022re3}, some generate a document section by section~\citep{shao2024assisting,bai2025longwriter,huot2025agents}, some recursively decompose the document into tasks~\citep{xiong2025beyond}, and some adapt plans or structural reasoning during generation~\citep{wan2025cognitive,sun2026cot}.
Across these systems, the design fixes what each step advances, whether a content unit, a task node, or a prescribed stage, so the writer does not decide whether the document next needs more planning, drafting, or reviewing.

The cognitive process theory of writing~\citep{flower1981cognitive,hayes1996new,hayes2012modeling} motivates a different unit of control.
\citet{flower1981cognitive} characterize writing as coordination among planning, translating, and reviewing, with a monitor deciding which process to invoke as the document develops.
Building on \citet{flower1981cognitive}, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, a long-form writing system that implements this process-level control as a writing skill that a coding agent loads, with persistent workspace state and subagent delegation (Figure~\ref{fig:agentic-cogwriter}).
A top-level Monitor agent observes the evolving draft, goals, and process history, chooses whether the next activity should be planning, drafting, or reviewing, and delegates that activity to a corresponding subagent.
We call the system \textit{agentic} for this combination: online selection of the next writing process, a persistent state shared across steps, and delegation of each selected process to a subagent.

We evaluate our system on three long-form writing benchmarks spanning broad instruction-driven writing, long-context generation, and methodical expert-document production~\citep{wu2026writingbench,que2024hellobench,malaviya2025dolomites}.
We hold the generator, harness, output budget, and task information constant across conditions.
Pointwise rubric scoring and pairwise same-prompt preference evaluation assess document quality from complementary perspectives.
In pre-specified per-benchmark pairwise tests, our system is preferred to one-shot generation in \PooledFourOneHolmCellsTotal\ of 9 tests and to a fixed writing pipeline and adaptive task planning in \PooledFourTwoHolmCellsTotal\ and \PooledFourThreeHolmCellsTotal, while a post-hoc prompt-level summary favors it over all three.
Adding our role instructions to the fixed pipeline post hoc does not reproduce this advantage, a second judge family prefers our system to that pipeline on one run, and a post-hoc test favors it over the plain agent except on DoLoMiTes.

The component analyses show that, on these tasks, the advantage over the fixed pipeline does not need explicit goals, adaptive process order, or subagents: dropping each stays near parity with our system, though the pre-specified single-writer variant trails it, and a post-hoc ablation dropping all three stays at parity and still beats the fixed pipeline.
Process traces show \AfourCycleCompliantRate\ of complete paths conform to the planning--drafting--reviewing cycle structure and \AfourReviewingToPlanningRate\ of runs replan.

Our contributions are threefold.
(1) We propose a writing skill for coding agents, and we compare our system with the plain agent and one-shot, fixed-stage, and task-planning systems; (2) a component analysis ties the advantage to a role-guided pass by the Monitor agent over a persistent draft, not to goals, adaptive order, or subagents; and (3) we release the skill, prompts, protocol, and code.
