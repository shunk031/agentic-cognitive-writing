\title{How Should an AI Agent Write? Process-Level Control for Long-Form Writing}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
AI agents increasingly produce long-form documents as part of multi-step knowledge work, but it remains unclear how the writing process itself should be organized. Existing systems typically advance an outline, a task graph, or a fixed sequence of stages. Drawing on cognitive process theory, we propose \condAgenticCogWriter, an architecture that maintains an evolving draft and writing state while a \texttt{Monitor} repeatedly chooses whether to plan, draft, or review and delegates that activity to a specialist. We evaluate the architecture on 300 demanding prompts from three complementary benchmarks spanning general-purpose writing, long-text generation, and structured expert documents. Independent rubric-based scoring ranks \condAgenticCogWriter\ highest on two of the three benchmarks. When a judge directly compares two responses to the same prompt, \condAgenticCogWriter\ is preferred to one-shot generation, a fixed planning--drafting--revision workflow, and adaptive task planning. Ablations show no significant degradation from removing the explicit goal network or fixing the process order. Trace analysis further shows that most runs follow a single planning--drafting--reviewing cycle. Together, the results support process-organized writing as a promising agent architecture while leaving its causal mechanism open.
\end{abstract}
