\title{What Makes a Writing Agent Work? Revisiting the Cognitive Process Theory of Writing with Language Agents}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Long-form writing agents typically organize generation around plans, sections, or tasks. We ask whether writing processes should instead form the controller's action space. \condAgenticCogWriter\ makes \texttt{Planning}, \texttt{Translating}, and \texttt{Reviewing} selectable actions coordinated by a \texttt{Monitor}. Across multiple long-form benchmarks, same-family pairwise judging prefers this process-organized design to \condSinglePass, \condStaged, and \condTaskPlanning. Yet removing the explicit goal representation or fixing process order produces no detectable loss, and the \texttt{Monitor} usually follows the fixed process cycle. Thus, the tested organization is preferred under the primary pairwise evaluation without identifying a single mechanism for the difference, illustrating how cognitive theory can yield falsifiable agent-design hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
