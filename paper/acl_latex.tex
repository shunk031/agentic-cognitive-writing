\title{Revisiting the Cognitive Process Theory of Writing for AI Agents: Process-Level Control for Long-Form Writing}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
AI agents increasingly produce long-form documents as part of multi-step knowledge work, but the writing process inside those agents remains under-specified. Existing writing systems typically advance an outline, a task graph, or a fixed sequence of stages. We ask whether an agent can instead choose what kind of writing work to perform next. We propose \condAgenticCogWriter, a long-form writing system inspired by cognitive process theory that maintains a shared draft, goals, and process history while a top-level agent chooses among planning, drafting, and reviewing and delegates the selected process to a subagent. We evaluate the system on demanding subsets of three long-form writing benchmarks. Pointwise rubric scoring ranks \condAgenticCogWriter\ highest on two benchmark types, and pairwise comparisons prefer it to one-shot generation, a fixed planning--drafting--revision workflow, and adaptive task planning. Pre-specified tests with multiple-comparison correction find no reliable difference when we remove the explicit goal representation or fix process order; exploratory analysis suggests only a small benefit from adaptive ordering. Execution traces show that most runs follow a single planning--drafting--reviewing cycle. The results support process-organized writing while leaving the main source of its advantage unresolved.
\end{abstract}
