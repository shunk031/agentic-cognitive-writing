\title{Revisiting the Cognitive Process Theory of Writing for AI Agents: Process-Level Control for Long-Form Writing}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
AI agents increasingly produce long-form documents as part of multi-step knowledge work, yet most writing systems still advance generation through an outline, task graph, or fixed stage sequence. Agentic CogWriter instead treats the next writing process as the unit of control. Inspired by cognitive process theory, the system maintains a shared draft, goals, and process history while a top-level agent chooses among planning, drafting, and reviewing and delegates the selected process to a corresponding subagent. We evaluate Agentic CogWriter on demanding subsets of three long-form writing benchmarks using pointwise rubric scores and direct same-prompt comparisons. The system achieves the strongest pointwise scores on two benchmark types and is preferred to one-shot generation, a fixed planning--drafting--revision workflow, and adaptive task planning in pairwise evaluation. Component tests leave the source of the improvement unresolved: removing explicit goals or fixing process order does not reliably change pairwise preference, although exploratory analysis suggests a small advantage for adaptive ordering. Observed writing trajectories usually contain a single planning--drafting--reviewing cycle. The results support process-level control as a promising design for agentic writing while leaving the source of its advantage open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Consensus-Writing Evaluation on Habermas Machine Data}
\label{app:habermas}
We additionally evaluate the writing systems on ten short consensus-writing tasks constructed from the Habermas Machine data. Each task provides one deliberation question and the five participants' original opinions, and asks the system to write a statement the group could endorse. The ten tasks are drawn from the \texttt{OOD\_TEST} split after requiring complete, distinct participant opinions and disagreement ratings on both sides of the scale midpoint. Across eight conditions, this produces 80 attempted runs; 78 complete successfully. Table~\ref{tab:appendix-habermas} summarizes pointwise quality and the available process statistics.
\input{tab/appendix-habermas}

\condExploratoryOne\ is the exploratory CogWriter-style baseline, with initial planning, immediate plan revision, parallel segment generation, and length review without a goal network; \condExploratoryTwo\ is the exploratory STORM-style baseline, with perspective discovery, simulated question answering, outlining, per-section drafting, and polishing without retrieval. On these short consensus-writing tasks, \condAgenticCogWriter\ does not achieve the highest pointwise composite; in particular, it scores below \condSinglePass\ and \condStaged. \condAgenticCogWriter\ and \condFixedOrder\ record \HabAfourRegenerated\ and \HabAsixRegenerated\ regeneration events, respectively; their ledgers contain \HabAfourLedger\ and \HabAsixLedger\ no/proposal outcomes, respectively.
